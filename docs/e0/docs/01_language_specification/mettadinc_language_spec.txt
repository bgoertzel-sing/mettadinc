Scope. A proposed MeTTa sublanguage whose core is graded: every adequacy question can be measured
as a defect or checked against a bound, and the crisp yes/no answers are the special case of a two-valued
algebra. Deterministic, stochastic and nondeterministic dynamics; enumerated, symbolic and space-
backed carriers; quotient, graded, simulation, linear and transfer arrows, each with its own registered
composition law; typed claims that say which notion of sameness was established; replayable evidence
throughout. Cetta is the proposed first host.
Validation package. A machine-readable registry of declarations and requests, complete fixture
modules for every example, a claims manifest, an exact-arithmetic oracle with its results, a static checker
that ties these together, and pinned tool versions. The listings in this document are excerpts of those
fixtures, and the registry and fixtures are authoritative where the text and they differ. A coding-agent
implementation guide of the same date accompanies this specification. No MeTTaDinC runtime exists
yet.

                                               Abstract
      An intelligent system keeps some distinctions and discards others, and whether a discarded
      distinction mattered depends on what the system will later ask and do. MeTTaDinC is
      a proposed MeTTa sublanguage that makes this dependence explicit and executable. A
      program declares carriers, observers, operations and candidate encodings; the language
      measures how much each encoding loses, explains the loss with replayable witnesses, repairs
      it, induces abstract operations, and composes the results. Observers are tolerances in the
      sense of the distinction calculus, so current adequacy, compatibility of encoded updates
      and behavioral adequacy are inequalities between tolerances, and each can be measured
      as a defect or checked against a bound. The behavioral tolerance is a greatest fixed point
      whose complement is the bisimulation pseudometric; for two-valued tolerances computing it
      is ordinary partition refinement.
           Several different answers to the question “what may be forgotten?” are legitimate, and
      the language keeps them apart as typed claims: literal equality and equality up to a declared
      observer; bisimulation, trace equivalence and quantitative trace discrepancy; simulation for
      sound over-approximation; exact and approximate induction of abstract machines; signed and
      positive linear realizations. A seven-state stochastic chain, a mandatory fixture, shows three
      of these answers disagreeing on the same states. Each arrow kind has its own composition
      law, including a norm-aware law for signed linear arrows, and hives come in two typed
      forms: kernel hives governed by metric coherence and transfer hives governed by response
      preservation up to a declared gauge. Carriers may be enumerated, symbolic or drawn from a
      pinned space snapshot; the symbolic path has an explicit admission contract, and a forty-bit
      fixture with eight behavioral classes, together with an adversarial candidate that covers only
      four of its words, keeps that contract in the first acceptance target.
           Hyperseed supplies the main ontology application, and six laboratories cover neural
      report audits, evolutionary archives, skill-transfer hives, regime repair, arithmetic observer
      discovery, and over-approximation of an access protocol. The final sections connect the
      design to finite OmegaPLN, MeTTaProN, ωPLN and MORK, and to the inspected source of
      Cetta at a pinned revision. The contribution is an integrated executable interface built from
      established constructions, with the distinction calculus supplying the organizing semantics.
      Keywords: distinction calculus; tolerances; behavioral pseudometrics; trace equivalence;
      partition refinement; lumpability; weighted automata; simulation; polynomial functors;
      hives; MeTTa; Cetta; Hyperseed.

1     Purpose and scope
MeTTaDinC is a proposed MeTTa sublanguage for building, comparing and transforming
observer-relative descriptions. Its central question is operational: which distinctions must a
representation keep so that the observations and operations a system will rely on stay meaningful,
and what does it cost, in error, to drop the others? A program declares a local domain, the
questions asked of it, the operations that may follow, and a candidate encoding. The language
measures what the encoding loses, explains the loss with explicit witnesses, repairs it, induces
abstract operations, and composes the resulting arrows with others that have already been
checked.
    The motivating users are agents that fork, merge, exchange memories, translate skills
and reorganize their own descriptions—Omega agents and hives in the first instance. Such
agents repeatedly need a notion of sameness fitted to a particular use. Literal equality keeps
irrelevant implementation detail; an unlabelled similarity score can erase a distinction that
a later operation needs. Between those extremes lie several precise notions, and an agent
that confuses them will draw wrong conclusions: two states can produce the same observation
sequences with the same probabilities and still differ in branching structure that an adaptive
test detects; a map can preserve every reward while relabelling every action. The distinction
calculus supplies a common mathematics for these decisions: observers as tolerances, dynamics
as pullbacks, refinement and abstraction as learning rules, brackets that measure what an
observer keeps, and hives of observers linked by correspondences [1, 2, 3]. MeTTaDinC turns a
selected part of that mathematics into inspectable executable objects, and it types the answers
so that each result says which notion of sameness it established.

1.1    What a MeTTaDinC program says
A typical interaction runs as follows. An agent has a memory of evidence packets and wants
to store only the current posterior. It declares the packet carrier, the posterior observer, the
update operations that add receipts, and the posterior-only encoding, then asks three questions.
Does the encoding answer the posterior query now? Do encoded states that agree continue
to agree after an update? Can any sequence of updates reveal a difference the encoding has
merged? Measured as defects, the answers are 0, 3/20 and 3/20: two packets with the same
posterior today can differ by 0.15 in posterior after one more receipt. Asked instead as checks,
the first succeeds and the other two are refuted with a witness that names the two packets, the
receipt and the two posteriors. The agent can accept the error, repair the encoding (four classes,
no error), or ask for the weakest encoding whose error stays below a chosen bound (two classes
at bound 3/20). Each answer carries evidence that a small independent checker can replay.
    The language therefore has two connected roles:
    1. a semantic workbench, in which researchers state finite thought experiments and receive
       exact, reproducible answers; and
    2. an agent interface, in which representations, observers, methods, costs and certificates
       become data that reasoning and control can use.
The first role can be implemented well before the second matures, so the worked examples in
this document are at the same time explanations and conformance targets.

1.2    Why a distinction calculus
The distinction calculus is the organizing semantics, and four of its ideas recur throughout this
specification.


                                                5
MeTTaDinC                                                     Specification draft, 4 November 2026


    Observers are tolerances. An observer on a carrier X is a table α : X × X → [0, 1], reflexive
and symmetric, recording how indistinguishable two states are to it: 1 means it cannot tell them
apart, 0 means it separates them completely. Write ᾱ = 1 − α for the corresponding distance.
A partition is the two-valued special case. The tolerance is metric when ᾱ is a pseudometric,
equivalently when it is transitive for the Łukasiewicz product a ⊗ b = max(0, a + b − 1); a
two-valued metric tolerance is an equivalence relation. A pseudometric may assign distance
zero to two different values, so an observer can regard distinct things as interchangeable; the
language records when a claim holds only up to the observer and when it holds literally.
    Dynamics pull observers back. A step T : X → X turns α into T ∗ α(x, y) = α(T x, T y), the
observer that looks one step ahead. When α ≤ T ∗ α, everything the observer identifies now
it still identifies after the step; the calculus calls α an emergent level of T . For a partition
this is exactly compatibility of the encoded update. The refinement rule α ← α ∧ T ∗ α adds
the distinctions the step creates; iterating it is partition refinement, and its graded version
computes the behavioral tolerance of Section 4.
    Brackets measure what an observer keeps. For a probability law µ, the bracket ⟨µ2 | α⟩ =
  x,y µ(x)µ(y)α(x, y) is the weakness of the observer, and ⟨µ | ᾱ⟩ its distinction mass (logical
P                                                            2

entropy for partitions). Along a run, the balance law says the weakness rises by what the
dynamics blurs and falls by what the observer learns [1, 2]. Weakness is the Occam quantity
the calculus optimizes, in the quantale form developed for AGI-25 [4].
    Hives join observers. A hive is a family of observers with correspondences between their
carriers. When the correspondences are kernels, the hive is coherent when the joined tolerance
is metric, and failure appears as associators and as holonomy around cycles. When the
correspondences are executable transfers between agents, coherence means that going around
a cycle returns each agent to itself up to a declared symmetry of what it protects. Transfer
libraries, evaluator panels, and the forks of a self-modifying agent are hives of one kind or the
other, and Section 10.4 keeps the two kinds distinct.

In plain terms. An observer is a judgment of which things look alike. MeTTaDinC asks
whether that judgment survives the operations a system performs, measures by how much it
fails, and offers the cheapest repair. The same vocabulary covers a memory summary, a neural
report, an evolutionary archive key, a skill translation and an algebraic invariant.

1.3   Place in the Hyperon stack
MeTTaDinC uses MeTTa syntax and is hosted first in Cetta, the direct C runtime for MeTTa
[5]. It is meant to share one finite core with neighbouring languages rather than duplicate them.
Finite OmegaPLN already has exact-rational semantics, compositional (L, η) error grades, a
posterior-only-summary counterexample and lumpability as stochastic descent [6]; MeTTaDinC
adopts that core and its reference evaluator as a differential oracle once an interpreter version
is pinned. MeTTaProN supplies probabilistic and neural kernels through a shared kernel IR
[8]. ωPLN consumes MeTTaDinC results through an explicit adapter: exact results become
exact channels, and an estimate becomes evidence only through a conversion that preserves its
estimand and its dependence assumptions. MORK and PathMap supply carriers defined by
queries over pinned space snapshots. Hyperseed and the typed proof-plan language consume
scoped claims [9, 10]. Section 14 specifies each interface.

1.5    Status of this document
This is a design specification for a language still to be implemented. Its normative parts are
the semantics in Sections 3–11 and the validation package that accompanies it: the registry
file registry/mettadinc_registry.json, which fixes every declaration, request, argument,
default, result field, status and profile; the fixture modules in fixtures/, which are complete
MeTTaDinC sources for every example; and the claims manifest (manifest.json), which
connects each claimed result to its fixture form and to the values that confirm it. The listings
printed here are excerpts of the fixtures, and the appendices are generated from the registry.
An exact-arithmetic oracle recomputes every number quoted in the worked examples and
the counterexamples behind the theorem hypotheses, and a static checker verifies that the
fixtures conform to the registry and that the manifest agrees with the oracle. Neither tool runs
MeTTaDinC. Claims about neural audits, search archives or agent coordination at scale are
hypotheses for empirical work.

2.1   Claims are about models
Every result names a claim, the model it concerns, the method that produced it, and its scope.
The model is an immutable snapshot of an admitted module: resolved carriers, observers with
their value tolerances, actions and their kinds, measures, cost models, imported library versions,
and dependencies. A model may itself be an empirical proposal, such as a transition table
estimated from logs; an exact result about it is exact about the table and says nothing by itself
about the system the table was fitted to. Conclusions about populations or real systems carry
their own empirical evidence and status.
    A result is also bound to the exact objects it is about. A certificate that is valid for one
machine is no evidence about another, even one with the same carrier, or with the same name
in another module, so every result records its binding: the subject declarations, by module
digest and name; for arrows, simulations and transfers, the identities of the source and target
models; and the interface it was checked against, meaning the queries with their value tolerances,
the actions with their update kinds, and the merges. Its scope states the initial states, the
reachable-state scope, the horizon, the discount and the policy class (fixed action words or
observation-based policies). The result also lists its assumptions, its provenance (the certificate
kind and digest, and the digests of the streams and snapshots it read) and its assurance, the
ground on which its status was assigned (Section 2.5). Appendix B lists the fields of this
envelope, and Section 2.8 states what an operation checks before it uses a result.

2.2   Statuses
Results take one of seven statuses (Table 2). A status qualifies one claim; it is not a quality
score for an object. Every request can return the four base statuses Conditional, Unknown,
Unsupported and InvalidInput, and the registry lists the further statuses each request can
return; Conditional is a base status because any result can rest on an unreplayed backend
answer, a declared assumption or a premise that is not Checked. One assurance policy assigns
the statuses (Section 2.5), and the result’s Assurance field records the ground on which it did.

 Checked          The claim is established within its scope by an exact method that the core runs on
                  the frozen model, or by a certificate that the core’s replay checker validates.
 Refuted          A counterexample or lower-bound certificate that the core replays by evaluating

2.4   Measuring and checking
Graded quantities are reported as certified intervals. A defect result carries [ℓ, u] with a
certificate for each end: a lower-bound certificate (a test whose evaluation shows the defect is at
least ℓ) and an upper-bound certificate (a prefixpoint with couplings or matchings, or stability
equations, showing it is at most u). Exact computation gives ℓ = u.
    Two request forms use these intervals differently, and the registry gives them different
names. A measurement (MeasureNow, MeasureUpdates, MeasureBehavioral, MeasureTrace,
Defects) asks for the value. It is Checked when the interval has width at most its Precision
argument (default 0, an exact value) and Unknown otherwise; the claim is that the defect lies
in the interval, so a measured defect of 3/20 is a successful measurement. A check (CheckNow,
CheckUpdates, CheckBehavioral, CheckTrace) asks whether the defect is at most its Within
argument, whose default is 0. It is Checked when u is at most the bound, Refuted when ℓ
exceeds it, with the lower-bound certificate as witness, and Unknown otherwise. Checking that


                                                  9
MeTTaDinC                                                      Specification draft, 4 November 2026


the same 3/20 defect is at most 1/10 is a refuted claim. A crisp yes/no check is the case of
bound 0 with defects in {0, 1}. Projections with Output are defined only on Checked results,
so a measurement can feed later computations whatever its value. An exact measurement, at
(Precision 0), needs a lower and an upper certificate whose values meet. A distance can be
the limit of values that no single test attains, and when no registered certificate reaches it the
correct result is Unknown with the certified interval (Section 4.4).

2.5   What is trusted
The trusted component is a small replay checker. A certificate is data: a separating pair and
test, a set of stability equations, a splitting tree, a characteristic-formula graph, a coupling, a
matching, a simulation relation, an intertwining matrix with residual bounds, an exact dual
solution of a linear program, a propositional proof log, or a typed derivation. The checker replays
it against the frozen model without calling the algorithm that produced it. An atom whose
head is Checked is ordinary data until the checker has validated its evidence and dependencies.
    One assurance policy gives each status a single operational meaning, the same in every
module and library. A result is Checked when its claim is established within its scope by an exact
method that the core runs on the frozen model—finite enumeration, exact rational arithmetic,
partition refinement, splitting trees replayed on representatives—or by a certificate that the
replay checker validates: a replayed witness, a coupling, a matching, stability equations, an
exact dual certificate of a linear program, or, for a propositional obligation that the obligation
generator built from the admitted source, a DRAT proof converted to LRAT and checked by
cake_lpr. The Assurance field records these two grounds as Exact and Replayed. Distance
values are held to the second ground: a behavioral or trace distance enters a result only through
the certificate kinds registered for it (Section 4.4), which bound it from both sides, so an
algorithm that computes the value exactly, such as the polynomial-time algorithm for chains
with one transition function, proposes the value and its certificates and does not by itself make
the result Checked. A verdict whose evidence is an external backend’s answer that the core has
not replayed, such as an SMT solver’s “unsat”, or a SAT solver’s “unsat” without a replayed
proof, is Conditional, with assurance TrustedBackend and the undischarged assumption
(TrustedBackend solver version logic obligation-digest)

for example (TrustedBackend cvc5 1.4.2 QF_LRA sha256:...). When a second backend
reaches the same verdict on an independently written encoding, the result records it in its
evidence under agreed_by; the agreement is evidence, and it does not discharge the assumption.
A result is Refuted, with assurance Witness, only when a counterexample or lower-bound
certificate has been replayed by evaluating the admitted source; a witness that a backend
found becomes a refutation once the core has replayed it. Estimated results rest on a declared
statistical procedure, with assurance Statistical. A result can also rest on an assumption that
the admitted source declares by name, for example the assumption, carried by the provenance
of a kernel imported with estimated masses, that the kernel is the law of the system it was fitted
to. A result computed exactly on such a kernel is Checked as a claim about the kernel; read
as a claim about the system, it is Conditional, with assurance Assumed and the assumption
(Assumption name digest), where name is the declaration that carries the assumption and
digest identifies its provenance record.
   The policy lets a consumer act on a status without asking how it was produced. Agreement
between two solvers on two encodings makes an error less likely, and the verdict is still an
answer the core has taken on trust; a consumer that acts only on reproduced evidence has to
be able to tell it apart from a verdict the checker reproduced, and the status does that. Which
Conditional results a consumer accepts is the consumer’s declaration (Section 2.7).

    Two further pieces sit inside the trusted boundary. For symbolic carriers, the obligation
generator that translates admitted terms and the frozen model semantics into proof obligations
(propositional formulas, matrix identities) is trusted: a proof log shows that a formula is
unsatisfiable, and only the generator connects that formula to the MeTTaDinC claim. Section 15
pins the initial pipeline and the guide specifies how the generator is tested. Second, the definitions

2.6    Horizons and budgets
A semantic horizon is part of a claim. The horizon (Steps h) covers the initial observation
and h transitions, so h + 1 observations; (Steps 0) checks the present observation only, and
AllFiniteWords covers every finite horizon. “Behavioral adequacy through four steps” and “for
all finite words” are different claims. A budget limits the work spent establishing a claim. When
a budget ends, the result is Unknown with a record of the work completed; it never becomes a
shorter-horizon claim after the fact. An exhaustive search over a declared finite family that
completes without a counterexample establishes agreement on that family and nothing beyond
it. Host evaluation fuel is a host limit and never counts as a semantic horizon.

2.7    Exact, estimated and conditional results
Consumers declare what they accept, with the claim kinds and result types they need. (Accepts
Checked) accepts only Checked results. A consumer may also accept Conditional results whose
only undischarged assumptions are trusted-backend assumptions for backends it names:
(Accepts Checked (Conditional (TrustedBackend cvc5)))

A consumer whose declaration lies outside the agent’s rewrite authority (held by the frame)
accepts only Checked results unless the external ceremony that governs it records otherwise;
a consumer in the agent’s own custody may list backends. An empirical consumer may
accept Estimated results under its own signature, (Accepts Checked Estimated), and the es-
timate then carries the fields of Section 7.4. Declared assumptions are accepted by name,
as in (Accepts Checked (Conditional (Assumption StepModel))), and the forms com-
bine: (Accepts Checked Estimated (Conditional (TrustedBackend cvc5) (Assumption
StepModel))) accepts a Conditional result only when each of its undischarged assumptions
is covered. No consumer accepts Unknown or partial payloads, and no status is accepted by
coercion.
    A result derived from premises carries the union of their assumptions. Lifting a statistic to
a keyed store, composing arrows with Then, transferring safety along a simulation, rebuilding
an index and evaluating a gate bundle are derivations of this kind, and a derivation from a
Conditional premise is Conditional whatever the method of its last step. A result derived from
an Estimated premise is Conditional on that estimate, with the assumption (EstimatedPremise
result-digest delta), which carries the estimate’s failure probability δ; it holds on the event
that the estimate’s guarantee holds, the failure probabilities of several such premises add, and
only a consumer that accepts Estimated results accepts it. A gate reads the status and the
assumptions of every result in its bundle and checks them against the declaration of each
consumer the change touches. A Conditional result that a consumer does not accept counts
as inadequate coverage for that consumer, never as Checked. Discharging an assumption is a
separate evidence-producing step, for example replaying a proof that the backend emitted, after

which the derivations that used the result are evaluated again.

2.8   Dependencies
Derivations are lazy and memoized against their complete input snapshot. A request evaluates
only the prerequisites it needs. When a prerequisite fails or is unresolved, the consumer reports
a dependency diagnostic naming it; the failure is never reported as a refutation of the consumer’s
own claim and never replaced by a default value. When an interface changes, a certificate is
reused only through the transport rules of Section 4.8, which distinguish universal bounds,
witnesses, exact values, optimality claims and measure-dependent results.
   An operation that consumes a result validates the result’s binding before it uses the result,
and a mismatch is InvalidInput naming the mismatched field; a successful status alone is never
enough. The duty applies to every consuming operation:
   • Then and the composition of transfers and contracts: the target of the first arrow is the
     source of the second, with the same interface identity;
   • safety transfer, (CheckSafety N (Never ...) (Via R) (Init ...)): the abstract
     machine of the simulation R is the request’s target N, and the result is about the concrete
     machine of R;
   • lifting an item statistic to a keyed store, (Keyed K (Of Store) (Item S)) with (Method
     Lift) in the live-memory library [26]: S is a statistic of the store’s item machine M, the
     machine named in the store’s Of. To index a view of the items, the declaration names the
     view, (View V): V has the state carrier of M and declares (ActionsOf M), S is a statistic
     of V, and the lifted result is bound to the queries of V only, so a consumer of the other
     queries of M gets no coverage from K;
   • rebuilds: the observed machine’s encoding and the read map’s codomain match the index;
   • streams and snapshots: a declared digest that does not match the content is InvalidInput,
     returned before any count, interval or estimate is computed, so a mismatching stream
     never yields an Estimated value;
   • gates: the bundle digests match the evaluated surface, and the binding of each result
     names the touched consumer’s machine and interface.
The theorems behind these operations, the lifting of an item statistic to a store and the safety
transfer of Proposition 8.1, hold only for the objects their hypotheses name. An evaluator
that checks the premise and the conclusion separately can combine a valid certificate about
one system with a claim about another: a statistic of a view with fewer queries, lifted as if it
belonged to the full item machine, gives the same lifted value to two stores that disagree on a
query the view lacks. Bound to the view’s queries, the same statistic still serves the consumers
of those queries, and a consumer added later for another query sees that it has no coverage.
Binding rules are admission rules: a request whose premises fail them is InvalidInput in every
release, including a release in which the request itself is Unsupported.

2.9   Numbers have types
Probabilities, tolerances and defects, p-bit evidence pairs, report availability, error bounds,
operator norms and costs are different types. The language converts between them only through
declared methods. In particular a p-bit is never read as a probability, a tolerance is never read
as a transition law, a checked transfer is never read as a tolerance kernel, and a host result
bag is read as a set, a multiset or a law only under an interpretation declared in the interface
(Section 8).

3     Semantic foundations
The semantics separates three things that are easy to run together: the interface a system offers
(what can be asked and done), a machine that implements it, and the observers that judge
which states look alike. Arrows between machines are typed by interfaces, and each arrow kind
has a registered composition law.

3.1    Interfaces and machines
Definition 3.1 (Interface). An interface consists of
    1. finite query labels J, each with a value type Yj carrying a metric value tolerance βj (crisp
       equality unless declared otherwise);
    2. finite action labels A, each with an update kind: deterministic, stochastic or nondetermin-
       istic;
    3. optionally, an availability guard giving the set A(y) ⊆ A of actions available when the
       observation tuple is y (all actions by default); and
    4. optionally, merge labels of arity two.
Query labels, value tolerances, action labels, update kinds and guards are all part of the
interface’s identity.

    The value distance 1 − βj is a pseudometric, so two different values may lie at distance zero.
Write y ∼0 y ′ when βj (y, y ′ ) = 1. This is an equivalence relation, because a metric tolerance is
transitive, and the quotient Yj /∼0 is the protected observation space of the query: the values
the observer can tell apart. The tolerance is separated when ∼0 is equality, as for the discrete
tolerance and for max(0, 1 − |p − p′ |) on rationals. The trivial tolerance β ≡ 1 is the extreme
non-separated case, whose protected space has one point. Separation is computed at admission
and recorded in the interface. Write [oj ] for the composite of oj with the quotient map onto
Yj /∼0 . Crisp statements below read ker oj as ker[oj ] and compare values of oj in Yj /∼0 ; for
separated tolerances this is literal equality.

   1. Pullback preserves tolerances, metric tolerances and crisp equivalences, and (g ◦f )∗ = f ∗ g ∗ .

The calculus’s event grammar has four kinds of event: evolution x 7→ T x, fork x 7→ (x, x),
merge (x, y) 7→ x ⋆ y, and erasure [2]. Agents that keep memories in several forks and later
reconcile them need merges as first-class operations. A crisp representation q is compatible
with a merge when qx = qx′ and qy = qy ′ imply q(x ⋆ y) = q(x′ ⋆ y ′ ); the graded condition is
αq (x, x′ ) ⊗ αq (y, y ′ ) ≤ αq (x ⋆ y, x′ ⋆ y ′ ), nonexpansiveness for the sum of the two input distances.

Lemma 3.11 (Merges reduce to translations). An equivalence relation P on X is compatible
with ⋆ if and only if it is compatible with every translation x 7→ x ⋆ c and x 7→ c ⋆ x, c ∈ X.

Proof. Necessity: take y = y ′ = c. Sufficiency: from xP x′ and yP y ′ , right translation by y gives
(x ⋆ y)P (x′ ⋆ y), left translation by x′ gives (x′ ⋆ y)P (x′ ⋆ y ′ ), and transitivity finishes.

   Merges therefore enter refinement as a family of unary actions, and every algorithm of
Section 4 applies to them unchanged; the price is one translation action per element (or per
symbolic class of elements). Section 10.5 gives the memory-merge fixture, where the posterior
summary fails this test and an independent-evidence merge double counts shared receipts.

4.4    Certificates for graded claims
Graded results carry two-sided certificates. A lower bound needs a test that separates; an upper
bound needs a fixed-point inequality. The form of each depends on the dynamics and on the
kind of claim.

Definition 4.9 (Tests). Tests are [0, 1]-valued functions on X built from: g ◦ oj with g 1-
Lipschitz on (Yj , 1 − βj ); 1 − φ; min(φ, φ′ ); max(φ, φ′ ); max(0, φ − r) for rational r; and
the next-step expectation ⟨a⟩γ φ(x) = γ ETa x φ (deterministic and stochastic), with the two
modalities γ maxTa x φ and γ minTa x φ for nondeterministic actions. A test whose next-step
operators are applied to tests that are themselves nonlinear in a probability is a modal test;
a test built from one action word and one observation event is a word test (deterministic) or
trace-event test (stochastic). A policy-tree test (stochastic) is the probability of one event on
complete histories of actions and observations under a finite policy tree, a tree whose nodes are
keyed by observed histories and name the action, or law on actions, chosen there; a trace-event
test is the case of a tree whose actions ignore the observations. Trace-event and policy-tree
tests are plain probabilities of an event, with no discount, and they serve as lower certificates at
discount one only. The depth of a test is the largest number of nested next-step operators, or
the depth of its tree.

4.6    Induction: exact and approximate
Inducing an abstract machine from a representation is two different operations with two different
contracts, and the language gives them different names.
    Exact induction, InduceExact R, discharges three obligations: current adequacy with
defect zero, update compatibility with defect zero, and literal congruence of the fibers of q,
which is checked on the code values themselves, independently of the code tolerance. Literal
congruence asks that states with the same code value have successors with the same code
value, q(x) = q(x′ ) ⇒ q(Ta x) = q(Ta x′ ), for deterministic actions; the same law on code values,
q∗ Ma (x, ·) = q∗ Ma (x′ , ·), for stochastic ones; and the same set of code values for nondeterministic
ones. For a crisp representation it is the update obligation itself. Under a code tolerance that
identifies distinct codes it is strictly stronger, and it is what makes the abstract updates
well defined on q(X). Current adequacy needs no such addition, because q(x) = q(x′ ) gives
αq (x, x′ ) = 1 whatever the code tolerance, and a zero current defect then yields decoders on the
code values. When the three obligations hold, Proposition 4.5, applied to the discrete equality
on q(X), gives the quotient machine with decoders and abstract updates, and q is a quotient
abstraction arrow, checked as such; the result records whether observation equality is literal or
up to the observer, and it carries the declared code tolerance as data on the abstract carrier.
When an obligation fails, the result is Refuted with the witness of the first failed obligation,
and a failure of literal congruence is witnessed by a CongruenceWitness: two states with the
same code value, an action, and their two successor code values (for laws, a code value and
its two masses). The regime laboratory of Section 13.4 is refuted on current adequacy and
the eight-state encoding that keeps only the first bit on update compatibility. The machine of
Section 4.2 with the indiscrete code tolerance is refuted on literal congruence, although both
graded checks pass:

    The trade-off involves a choice. The equivalence relations below a non-transitive threshold

declared family completes, and is otherwise Unknown with the best value found. The defect
kind is declared: behavioral diameter in dγbeh , pair-mass defect under a pair law, total-variation
lumpability defect [42, 41], or the Kullback–Leibler divergence rate of the aggregated chain, for
which spectral and information-bottleneck methods exist [45, 46]. Weakness may be computed

because the coarsest representation is often not the cheapest to maintain [20].

5.2   Typed carriers and name resolution
Every carrier states its element type, and the elements of an Enum are resolved at that type. In
a carrier whose element type is Symbol or a declared symbol enumeration, an atom denotes
itself. In any other carrier, an atom must name a constant of the element type and denotes
its value; anything else is InvalidInput. Thus the packet carrier of Section 12.4 declares four
symbols, and the candidates of the arithmetic laboratory declare two constants of a record
type, with no ambiguity about which reading is meant. Inside expressions, (Value KA) still
dereferences a constant explicitly. Carrier enumerations reject duplicates under typed canonical
equality, and Product enumerates in the declared component order.
    Records have named typed fields, each present exactly once. Sum types have named con-
structors with ordered typed arguments, and Variant names both the type and the constructor.
Optionality is an explicit sum. BoundedString and BoundedList bound sizes and check element
types; exact rationals are written (Rat p q) and normalized; floating-point literals are rejected
in exact positions.

5.4   Maps, bindings and the admitted pure fragment
Every core map is unary; a function of several arguments takes one product value. Cases
contains linear patterns, each variable bound once, scoped to its case. Every input must match
exactly one case: missing and overlapping cases are rejected, even when overlapping cases agree,
so that source order and host reduction multiplicity never become part of a map’s meaning.
Body binds its single argument lexically. Table lists input–output pairs and must be total and
single-valued on the declared domain. Choice lists the successor sets of a nondeterministic map.
    Admitted expressions are literals, constructors, field access, typed map application and
registered pure primitives, each total on its declared input type or returning an explicit tagged
failure value. The map-call graph is acyclic in the first exact fragment. Exact maps have no
access to mutable spaces, clocks, random sources, the network or unrestricted host evaluation.
An external adapter may capture data outside this boundary and supply a frozen table with
provenance; the checker then certifies only the table’s finite obligations.
   Observers can be generated as typed syntax, as in the arithmetic laboratory (Section 13.5):
Expr constructs held syntax, generation substitutes a bound field element for a variable by
capture-avoiding substitution, and the remaining variable is bound when the observer is applied.
The result is a program value that can be inspected, checked for invariance, compiled and
evaluated. Constructing it establishes no property of it.

7.1     Laws and lumping
A finite rational transition kernel Ma : X → DQ (X) has nonnegative rational rows summing to
one. It describes the law of a successor; it is not a tolerance. Deterministic updates are the
kernels with point-mass rows.
    Stochastic machines support two adequacy claims beyond the current and update checks, and
they are different claims (Section 4.3). Behavioral adequacy compares states in the Kantorovich
bisimulation pseudometric. Trace adequacy compares the joint labelled traces

only is a third, different query contract and is declared as such.

rows (q∗ Ma )(x, c) = y:qy=c Ma (x, y) agree within each fiber. With current adequacy it yields

the right abstraction records that mixture. Predictive-state models and observable-operator

    Deciding trace equivalence. MinimalLinear M computes the space spanned by the test
functions x 7→ Px (y0 a1 y1 · · · ak yk ) by Krylov iteration with exact rationals, its dimension,
a basis of tests, a signed realization in the sense of Definition 3.8, and for any requested
inequivalent pair a separating test. This is equivalence of weighted automata over the alphabet
of action–observation letters, decidable in polynomial time [48, 49, 50]; because the space grows
at each length until it stabilizes, a separating test with at most n − r actions exists, where r
is the number of observation values present. Equality under all action words implies equality
under every controller that uses the observed history, so unbounded trace equivalence is a
supported, Checked-or-Refuted claim.
    Quantitative trace discrepancy. Through a finite horizon the trace defect is computed exactly
by enumerating observation sequences under each word, at a cost exponential in the horizon
and charged to the budget. Over all horizons the total variation distance between two labelled
Markov chains can be approximated to arbitrary precision, but deciding whether it exceeds a
given threshold is NP-hard [36]. MeasureTrace without a horizon therefore reports a certified
interval, with lower bounds from trace-event witnesses and upper bounds from behavioral
certificates at discount one (Proposition 4.7); that the undiscounted bisimilarity distance bounds
this total variation distance is known [36], and the proposition states it in the form the replay
checker needs, with horizons and controllers. In the first stochastic release, trace requests
require discrete value tolerances.
    A policy that reacts to what it observes can choose, at each step, the action that best
separates two states given what it has seen, so over (Policies ObservationBased) the defect
can exceed the defect over words, and its refutations need their own witness. Through a finite
horizon the policy-class defect is computed exactly by enumerating deterministic policy trees: a
randomized policy separates no more, because its law is a mixture of the laws of deterministic
policies and total variation is convex under mixtures. A refutation is a PolicyTreeWitness:
the pair, a finite tree whose nodes are keyed by observed histories and name the action taken

7.4   Estimated results
An Estimated result names the estimand (a population quantity), the estimator, the sampling
protocol, the sample size n, an error bound ε with failure probability δ, the dependence
assumptions of the sample, and the rule used to combine several estimates. Simultaneous claims
combine by a union bound unless a joint procedure is declared. Monitors that look at a stream
repeatedly, such as regime detectors, use anytime-valid procedures—confidence sequences or
nonnegative test processes—rather than fixed-n bounds [82, 83, 15]. Exact arithmetic on a
sample never turns a population conclusion into an exact theorem.
    An estimate describes particular units observed under a particular policy, and the result
says which. Besides the fields above, an Estimated result names its population, the units the
stream samples; its selection rule, which units become rows and when; the policy version under
which the rows were produced, as a digest; and the estimand as a function of that population.
With the fields above, they make up the Estimate field of the result envelope (Section 5), and
they decide whether the estimate survives a change. A new policy can change which units are
selected as well as how they are labelled, and a bound on a rate among selected units need
not survive even a change that only removes units. If a policy proposes ten keys of which one
is a duplicate, the duplicate rate among proposed keys is 1/10; a policy that withholds the

                                               42
MeTTaDinC                                                      Specification draft, 4 November 2026


nine other keys proposes a subset whose duplicate rate is 1. Reusing an Estimated result under
a different policy version therefore needs a declared transport argument or a new estimate.
Pointwise monotonicity of the labels on a fixed population is such an argument: over the ten
keys, the rate of keys for which a duplicate was proposed can only fall when proposals are
withheld, and here it stays 1/10. If the selection rule depends on the policy, the result does not
transport.
    One trap deserves a fixture. Distinction masses are defined under the pair law µ⊗µ, diagonal
included. The plug-in estimate from a sample x1 , . . . , xn averages d(xi , xj ) over all n2 index
pairs; the n pairs with i = j contribute zero, so its mean is (1 − 1/n) times the estimand. The
unbiased U-statistic averages over i ≠ j [80]. It drops pairs with equal indices, not pairs with
equal values: dropping xi = xj changes the estimand to the distinct-pair quantity. For the
XOR report of Section 13.1, with D = 1/2 and n = 4, the plug-in mean is 3/8, the U-statistic
mean is 1/2, and the distinct-pair quantity is 2/3. Hoeffding’s inequality for U-statistics of
order two uses ⌊n/2⌋ in place of n [81]. The same structure governs conversion to PLN evidence
(Section 14): an estimate is not a count of independent observations just because it has a sample
size.


8     Nondeterminism, simulation and over-approximation
8.1    The nondeterministic profile
MeTTa evaluation is nondeterministic, and so are most of the sources an agent must reason
about without probabilities: a language model’s reports, an adversary, a user, a policy that
has not been learned yet. The nondeterministic profile gives such sources their own semantics
instead of forcing a probability onto them.
   Updates are maps Ta : X → P + (X). A host result bag becomes data of this profile only
under a declared interpretation: (Bag Set) reads it as the set of possible outcomes, (Bag
Multiset) keeps multiplicities with a declared meaning, and a law is obtained only through a
declared scheduler. The interpretation is part of the interface identity.
    Bisimulation is computed by relational partition refinement in O(m log n) [30], the simulation
preorder in O(mn) [55], and the graded versions use the Hausdorff lifting of Section 4.1 with
the two-sided matchings of Proposition 4.10 as upper certificates. Inequivalence witnesses
are distinguishing Hennessy–Milner formulas [56, 57]; computing minimal ones is NP-hard in
general, while useful variants are tractable [58]. May-trace equivalence is PSPACE-complete
[59]; antichain and up-to-congruence algorithms handle it well in practice [60, 61], and the
language reports it under a budget.
    Shared and fresh reads. Whether two reads of one nondeterministic or random variable
return the same value is a semantic choice, and it changes what observers can see. Let a program
read one choice c ∈ {0, 1} twice. With a shared read the outcomes are {(0, 0), (1, 1)}; with
fresh reads they are all four pairs. An observer of the first read cannot tell the programs apart;
an observer of agreement can, returning {true} against {true, false}, and under a declared fair
scheduler the agreement probabilities are 1 and 1/2. This is the sharing deformation of [11],
which governs overlapping evidence in PLN [12] and double counting in merges (Section 10.5).

8.2   Simulation arrows and sound over-approximation
An exact quotient of a real system is usually refuted: some rarely used path distinguishes states
the abstraction must merge. For security work the useful question is weaker and safer. Does
an abstraction that has more behaviors than the system still satisfy the property? If so, the
system does too, provided the abstraction is asked about the right states.

Proposition 8.1 (Safety transfers along simulations). Let R be a simulation arrow from M to
N (Definition 3.7) with the equality order on observations, let IM be the initial states of M , and
let B be a set of observation values. Suppose IM ⊆ dom(R), and let the abstract initial scope be
any set IN with R(IM ) ⊆ IN . If every state of N reachable from IN shows no value in B, then
every state of M reachable from IM shows no value in B. The same holds for every property
built from observation predicates, conjunction, disjunction and the universal next-step modality.

Proof. By induction along a run of M from x0 ∈ IM : coverage gives z0 with (x0 , z0 ) ∈ R and
z0 ∈ IN ; each step is matched by a step of N that keeps the pair in R, and related states show
equal observations. The universal fragment is closed under this matching.

    Without the coverage hypothesis the proposition is false: the empty relation is a simulation,
the abstract states reachable from the images of the initial states form the empty set, and every
property holds there vacuously, even when a concrete initial state violates it. CheckSafety
checks coverage first, and when it fails the result is Unknown with a diagnostic naming the
uncovered initial states (fixture hypotheses.metta). The proposition is also about one pair
of machines, the two that R relates. (CheckSafety N ... (Via R)) therefore requires the
abstract machine of R to be N, and a request whose target is another machine is InvalidInput,
returned before any exploration: the safety of a machine that R does not relate to the source
says nothing about the source (Section 2.8). The Simulation library registers three property
forms: (Shows o v), no reachable state shows value v for query o; (EnterFrom t (Not s)),
no reachable transition enters a state showing t from a state showing neither t nor s; and
(ReachWithin t k), no run of at most k steps from an initial state reaches a state showing t.
All three lie in the fragment of Proposition 8.1.
   Simulation arrows are the relational form of abstract interpretation [62]: a Galois connection
between sets of concrete states and abstract values determines a best abstract transformer, and

                                                44
MeTTaDinC                                                          Specification draft, 4 November 2026


its graph is a simulation. Graded simulations use directed relations, whose distance form is a
Lawvere quasi-metric; their grades add under composition. The witness-driven repair loop is
counterexample-guided abstraction refinement [63]: when a property fails on the abstraction, the
checker tries to concretize the abstract counterexample; a concrete run refutes the property, and
a spurious one yields Unknown together with a separating predicate that refines the abstraction.
The access-protocol laboratory (Section 13.6) runs this loop on a small protocol.

10     The composition kernel: polynomials, wiring and hives
10.1    Interfaces as polynomials, arrows as maps of lenses
Section 3 read an interface as a polynomial pI and a machine as a lens XyX → pI . Two
consequences shape the implementation. First, composite systems are built by the operations
of Poly: the parallel product of interfaces, and wiring lenses that feed some machines’ outputs
into others’ inputs. A hive of agents, an evaluator panel and a pipeline of memory, inference and
action modules are all wiring diagrams of machines [67]. Second, the arrows of Table 4 share
one typed infrastructure for composition: it checks that the middle object of a composite has
one interface identity, dispatches to the composition law registered for the arrow kind, computes
the composite data and grade, and records a dependency on both premise certificates. Each
law is a proved statement—additive defects for graded and transfer arrows, the norm-aware law
of Proposition 3.9 for linear arrows, the affine law for implementation contracts—and libraries
that add arrow kinds register a law with its proof. Composing arrows of different kinds is
InvalidInput unless a registered conversion exists.

10.3    Generic refinement and explanation
The refinement and witness requests for different dynamics are instances of one algorithm.
Coalgebraic partition refinement runs in quasilinear time for a class of functors that covers

represented as a directed acyclic graph in which subformulas are shared [66]; expanding a
separate formula for each inequivalent pair can be much larger, so the certificate format of
Section 4.4 stores the shared graph.

act partition refinement (quasilinear for the covered functors); generation of modal explanations
(quasilinear in the shared representation); computation of quantitative behavioral distances
(polynomial for labelled Markov chains with one transition function, no bound claimed for the
multi-action operator, Section 7); and replay of quantitative upper and lower certificates (linear
in the size of the certificate). An implementation may use one generic engine with per-functor
signature interfaces for the first two.

Proof. (1) Exactness gives νi (φy, m) = νj (y, ιm), so cij (x, y) = 1 exactly when x and φy have

Example 10.5 (Parity triangle). Three members each test one bit with the sharp tolerance
and the uniform law, with uniform weights. The correspondences c12 and c23 identify equal
values and c31 identifies opposite values. Each correspondence is a perfect identification, but
jointly they demand odd parity around the cycle: the holonomy from member one is the swap,
so the hive is incoherent. Closure identifies everything, and its consensus cost is the hive’s whole
distinction mass, 1/2. The least coherent graded refinement costs 1/6, attained by keeping a
perfect matching of the six identification edges [2, Example 9.6]. Optimality over all graded
refinements is a linear-programming fact, and it has an exact certificate. A linear program that
maximizes has a dual program that minimizes, and the value of every feasible solution of the
dual bounds the maximum from above; a feasible solution of each with the same value therefore
proves both optimal. Here the six identification edges form one cycle through the six values; a
dual solution of the program puts weight 1/2 on the transitivity constraint along each pair of
consecutive edges of the cycle, and its value 3 bounds the mass that any coherent refinement can
keep. The matching keeps mass 3, so both are optimal, and the cost is 2(6 − 3)/36 = 1/6. The
oracle checks the matching against all 120 transitivity constraints and the dual solution against
every variable in exact rational arithmetic, which is the replay a LeastCoherentRefinement
result’s optimality certificate receives.

11     Observer discovery
A failed check often means the interface lacks a question, not that the encoding is wrong.
Observer discovery proposes the question, proves it legitimate, and adds it.

11.1    Generated families and invariance
An ObserverFamily generates typed observer expressions from a finite parameter domain
(Section 5). SeekSeparator evaluates the family on a pair of candidates in a declared order
under a budget; CheckObserverInvariance proves, by structural rules on the expression,
that each generated observer is invariant under a declared equivalence—for matrix records,
simultaneous conjugacy of the marked fields, under which subtraction, scaling and multiplication
are covariant and rank is invariant. Evaluation and invariance are separate certificates, and
a separator refutes an equivalence only with both. Exhausting a family without a separator
establishes agreement on that family; an interrupted search is Unknown. A returned observer can
be added to an enriched interface, against which old encodings are rebound as new representation
objects and checked again.

11.2    Decision procedures as observer sources
Enumerating probes is weak where complete methods exist. An ObserverSource registers a
decision procedure together with its certificate formats, and its results enter the same evidence
protocol. For tuples of matrices, simultaneous conjugacy is isomorphism of modules over the
algebra the matrices generate, and it is decidable in polynomial time [75, 76]. When the modules
are isomorphic the procedure returns a conjugator P , replayed by checking P Ai P −1 = Bi and
invertibility. When they are not, a sound certificate is a mismatch of Hom dimensions: if
KA =∼ KB then dim Hom(KA , KB ) = dim End(KA ), each dimension being the nullity of a linear
system replayed by one rank computation. For the two candidates of the arithmetic laboratory
(Section 13.5), dim End(KA ) = 6 and dim Hom(KA , KB ) = 5 over F7 , which separates them
without a probe search. The arithmetic Langlands study already uses invertible intertwiners as

11.3    Cheap screens and their scope
Two screens are cheap and often useful, and both have precisely stated scopes. A cone-of-
influence pass reads dependencies from map syntax and drops coordinates that no query or
update can reach. Clock rates measure how often flipping a coordinate changes the task response.
Their guarantees, from the task-relevance study [3], hold under two hypotheses: the world
law is a product of Bernoulli laws with full support, and the predicate library consists of the
coordinate projections. Under those hypotheses:
  • the coordinates with positive clock rate form the unique minimal exact coordinate observer;
  • three rates are distinguished—the mean clock rate ri = Eµ d(W, Fi W ), the sup-norm rate
    Li = maxw d(w, Fi w), and the Brier rate τi —and the excess Brier risk of dropping a set
    U of coordinates lies between maxi∈U τi and i∈U τi , while the sup-norm rates give the

    corresponding bounds on cell diameters;
  • certified selection under an additive budget on these sufficient certificates is sorting by
    rate when every coordinate has unit cost, and a 0/1 knapsack problem when costs differ;
  • below the size of the minimal exact observer, the ranking by rate can be poor: two bits of
    a parity have large clock rates and jointly resolve nothing.
Outside these hypotheses the statements do not transfer. With predicates that are not coordi-
nates, a parity of many coordinates can be a single observer even though every participating
coordinate has positive clock rate, as the forty-bit parity machine shows; under dependent
laws a flip can leave the support. ClockRates therefore records the hypotheses it relied on
in its certificate and reports Estimated results for sampled rates. Screens propose candidates;
refinement certifies them.

11.4    Cost-aware choice
Separating observers differ in cost. In the arithmetic study a second invariant, rank(T3 + 4T13 −
2I), also separates the candidates but needs the operator T13 ; which one is cheaper depends
on what has already been computed [18]. Observers therefore carry acquisition and evaluation
costs, and observer choice uses the frontier of Section 4.7 with cost in the objective. Separation
alone never establishes cost optimality.

12.2    P-bits, availability and their algebras
A p-bit is a pair (p, n) ∈ [0, 1]2 of support and opposition, rational in the exact profile. The four
corners are true (1, 0), false (0, 1), both (1, 1) and neither (0, 0). The square carries two orders,
the bilattice structure of Belnap–Dunn logic [77, 78]: the truth order, in which (p, n) ≤t (p′ , n′ )
when p ≤ p′ and n ≥ n′ , and the knowledge order, in which (p, n) ≤k (p′ , n′ ) when p ≤ p′
and n ≤ n′ . The language names each operation by its order: PBitNot swaps the coordinates;
PBitAnd and PBitOr are the truth-order meet (min, max) and join (max, min); EvidenceJoin
is the knowledge-order join (max, max); and EvidenceProduct is the componentwise product
of the Hyperseed product quantale, whose unit (1, 1) differs from the truth-like corner [9].
    Connectives do not license inference rules by their names. With the designation p ≥ 1/2,
take A = (1, 1) and B = (0, 0): both A and the material implication ¬A ∨ B = (1, 0) are
designated while B is not. This is the familiar failure of disjunctive syllogism in Belnap–Dunn
and LP logics. An inference library must declare a vetted consequence relation—first-degree
entailment, LP, K3, or a logic of logical bilattices with its own implication, which validates
modus ponens [79]—and cannot invent one from connective names.

12.3    Contexts and consumer-relative substitution
A context in the manuscript is C = (XC , ΠC , δC , ιC ): a presented carrier, a presentation, and
p-bit evaluators of distinctness and interchangeability, which need not be negations of each
other [9]. Two receipt packets can be distinct as receipts and interchangeable for the current
posterior query; the two scoped predicates do not conflict. A p-bit entry in a context is an
asserted or computed evaluation. Whether substitution preserves future use is a claim about a
declared consumer machine, checked by the core, and the result says which claim kind—current,
behavioral or trace adequacy—was established and whether equality is literal or up to the
consumer’s observer.

and evidence requirements. Provenance, freshness, cost, permission status and guard values
are protected only if the consumer interface declares them; a property outside the interface is
outside every certificate. A guarded operation is totalized with a tagged outcome, such as (Type
Outcome (Sum (Ok State) (Denied Reason) (Failed Reason))), and lifted to an endomap
of the tagged carrier with a declared convention for what follows Denied or Failed (absorbing,
recovery, or retry); the convention is part of the interface.

12.8    Cycles and reflective structures
Mutually defined concepts and reflective selves are represented by finite graph records with stable
identifiers; reading a self-reference returns a reference. A finite graph equality or bisimulation
procedure may compare selected observations of such records, with its functor and equivalence
declared. Cyclic data are not circular proofs: a theorem certificate needs a well-founded
derivation or an admitted coinductive rule, and placing two unsupported claims in a cycle
establishes neither.

14     Integration with the Hyperon stack
The case for hosting MeTTaDinC in MeTTa rests on its connections. Each interface below is
a typed, explicit adapter; none converts silently between numeric meanings. A dependency
becomes usable by a milestone only when the validation package pins it (Section 16.2); until
then the interface is a design.

14.1    One finite core with finite OmegaPLN
Finite OmegaPLN has a four-sort language—raw evidence packets, chart handles with an exact
raw complement, estimates carrying an ideal recipe, a dependency set and an error grade, and
binary kernels—with an exact-rational reference interpreter, compositional (L, η) grades, the
contextual-quotient theorem showing that posterior-only summaries fail future-merge tests,
and lumpability as stochastic descent [6]. These are MeTTaDinC’s receipt-packet memory, its
affine contracts and its stochastic core under other names. The two languages should share one
finite semantic library—carriers, laws, tolerances, grades and their exact arithmetic—and the
OmegaPLN reference interpreter should serve as a differential oracle on the shared fixtures, once
a version of it is pinned with its interface and a compatibility test. A disagreement between
them is then a bug in one of them, triaged before either is released.

14.2    MeTTaProN kernels through a shared kernel IR
MeTTaProN specifies probabilistic and neural programs [8]. MeTTaDinC consumes its ker-
nels through a shared kernel IR rather than defining a parallel syntax: a declaration of the
form (TransitionKernel Step (From X) (To X) (Import MeTTaProN step-model)) names
a pinned kernel. The IR records the carriers, the masses (exact rationals or certified intervals),
normalization status, shared or fresh annotations for random choices, and provenance (model,
checkpoint, estimator). Exactness travels with the kernel: an exact kernel supports exact
MeTTaDinC claims about it; an estimated or sampled kernel supports Estimated claims, or
exact claims about the kernel that become Conditional, with the assumption (Assumption
name digest) its import declares, when they are read as claims about the system it was fitted
to (Section 2.5); a neural kernel from a frozen checkpoint enters with interval masses and its
checkpoint identity.

14.3    An adapter for ωPLN
ωPLN cannot use a certificate that refuses every conversion, so the adapter is explicit and
one-way, and each conversion states what it preserves.


                                                66
MeTTaDinC                                                     Specification draft, 4 November 2026


  • Checked and Refuted results become exact channels: exact constraints or truth values
    with exact provenance, carrying their claim kind.
  • An Estimated proportion becomes evidence counts only when it was estimated from n
    independent observations of the same estimand, population and policy version; the counts
    are the observed successes and n.
  • A U-statistic of order two is not n independent observations. It converts to an interval
    truth value from its declared concentration bound, or to counts with effective size ⌊n/2⌋
    when the consumer’s update rule is a Hoeffding-type bound.
  • An anytime-valid estimate, or an estimate selected by a sequential procedure, converts to
    an interval truth value at its stopping time and never to counts.
  • An estimate from dependent samples converts to an interval truth value with its declared
    dependence assumptions attached.
  • Conditional results become channels keyed by their assumptions; a result Conditional on
    an Estimated premise keeps that premise’s failure probability. Unknown, Unsupported
    and InvalidInput contribute nothing.
The fidelity analysis of ωPLN [7] can then account for what MeTTaDinC contributed.

14.4    Space data
Carriers and maps may be defined by queries over a pinned snapshot of a MORK or PathMap
space (Section 5.3). The content digest is the semantic identity; the in-process space revision is
a cache key, as in Cetta’s answer table, which already keys stored answers by space instance
and revision. For a crisp representation, whose code tolerance is the discrete equality, the
current and update checks are functional-dependency tests—does the code determine the query,
does the code determine the successor’s code—and run as group-by queries that PathMap
executes natively, in time linear in the carrier. Pairwise formulations belong to definitions, not
algorithms.

14.5    Proof plans and claims
MeTTaDinC results enter the Hyperseed proof-plan registry as warrants that keep their status,
claim kind, scope and dependencies [10]. A plan step that needs an abstraction cites a Checked
InduceExact result, or a Checked InduceApprox result together with its defect; a step that
relies on an estimate cites an Estimated result and inherits its error guarantee.

    Default algorithms are the efficient classical ones, used within their proved scope: functional-
dependency group-bys for the current and update checks of crisp representations (discrete
code tolerance), and the same group-bys on the classes of a two-valued metric code tolerance;
Hopcroft or Paige–Tarjan refinement for Pbeh followed by a dependency check, rather than
pair-graph search, for behavioral adequacy; splitting trees for minimal separating sequences
and coarseness certificates of deterministic machines [31]; O(m log n) lumping for stochastic
machines [43, 44]; Krylov iteration for trace equivalence and linear realizations; and symbolic
refinement with decision diagrams as in Sigref and Storm [69, 70]. The pair graph remains in
the definitions and in the reference engine.

15.3    The symbolic proof boundary
A symbolic certificate replays without the producing library, and its trusted base is stated
explicitly. The admission obligations of Section 5.3 and the constancy and stability equations of
a symbolic quotient are emitted as propositional formulas by an obligation generator that reads
the admitted terms and the frozen semantics of the registered primitives. A proof that a formula
is unsatisfiable says nothing about whether the formula expresses the MeTTaDinC obligation, so
the generator is part of the trusted base: it is small, it is versioned with the registry, its version
is recorded in every certificate it contributes to, and it is tested by translation validation on
scaled widths, where the obligations can also be decided by enumeration. A verified generator
is an open problem (Section 16).
    The initial pipeline is pinned. The solver CaDiCaL at release rel-3.0.1 [71] writes a
textual DRAT proof; drat-trim at commit 2e3b2dc0ecf938addbd779d42877b6ed69d9a985
checks it and writes an LRAT proof with its -L option [72]; and cake_lpr at commit
033aad53d1733bd4b8ca41cf6f1b5fb5d84f8de2, a checker verified in CakeML that accepts
LRAT and its LPR extension, checks the LRAT proof against the formula and prints s
VERIFIED UNSAT [73, 74]. The formats are not interchangeable without the conversion step:
cake_lpr does not read DRAT. Only the obligation generator and cake_lpr are trusted; the
solver and the converter may be replaced, for instance by a solver that writes LRAT directly,
without changing the trusted base. That cake_lpr commit is announced as its final release,
with a successor checker planned in the same repository; adopting the successor is a pin change
with its own fixture run. A verdict that reaches the result without this check, for example
an unsatisfiability answer from a solver whose proof was not replayed, is Conditional on that
solver (Section 2.5). For maps linear over a finite field, obligations are matrix identities replayed
by multiplication, and coarseness uses a splitting tree whose tests are replayed on concrete
representatives (Proposition 4.12).

15.4    Compilation
Specialized execution is planned to follow Cetta’s guarded supercompilation design—CettaPacks
with a multi-geometry IR and a guard lifecycle, and their approximate extension with explicit
error witnesses [24]—for the kernels that dominate cost: pair and splitting-tree traversal,
signature construction, sparse block sums, Krylov iteration and finite-field elimination, once that
design is available at a pinned version. Generated language packs can orchestrate requests where
the finite-Horn projection applies, and the LLVM emitter suits pure wrapper equations. Every
specialized path is checked by the same replay as the reference path, and every performance
claim compares paths with the same semantics and arithmetic.

16     Validation and research agenda
16.1    The first release
The first useful release is the graded deterministic core with its crisp special case, on both the
table and the symbolic backend, as fixed by the capability manifest in the registry: admis-
sion with the symbolic obligations, the measurement and check requests for current, update,
behavioral and trace defects, the behavioral tolerance, refinement with ledgers, exact and ap-
proximate induction with the literal-congruence obligation, the frontier on enumerated carriers,
CheckAbstractionMap, Then, CheckBlockPartition, the GradedKernels library, two-sided
and splitting-tree certificates with independent replay, the result envelope with its binding and
the assurance policy of Section 2, and the transport rules of Table 7. Its acceptance fixtures
are the eight-state calibration, the receipt-packet memory, the forty-bit machines with the
adversarial candidate and the reachability scope, the deterministic hypothesis modules and
the graded-closure module: 45 manifest claims. The same release must return Unsupported
for the other 78, including every request on the seven-state chain and on the two stochastic
modules of certificate_limits.metta, and the conformance suite checks those answers too.
The binding rules apply in that release as well, so a request whose premises are bound to the
wrong objects is InvalidInput there even when the request itself is Unsupported. Later stages
add the stochastic and linear core, nondeterminism and simulation, the composition kernel with
hives and merges, and the Hyperseed, discovery and integration libraries, in the order given by
the guide.

16.2    The validation package
The package that accompanies this specification contains the registry, twenty-one fixture files

16.3   Near-miss tests
The most informative tests separate claims that sound alike:
  • behavioral adequacy holds while encoded updates fail;
  • a behavioral check is refuted while every trace law agrees, and only a modal test explains
    it;
  • a zero current defect holds up to the observer but no literal decoder exists;
  • a horizon-one quotient fails as a stationary machine, and horizon h means h transitions;
  • a refutation on a carrier disappears on a forward-closed subset;
  • a zero-defect map is a homomorphism that is not a quotient;
  • the empty relation is a simulation and proves no safety property;
  • blocks that pass every local check silently omit part of the carrier;
  • equal stochastic laws disagree under a shared random seed, and equal expectations hide
    different laws;
  • a trace-equivalence partition has no Markov quotient, while a five-state Markov kernel
    abstraction preserves all trace laws;
  • two signed linear arrows with small residuals compose to a large one;
  • a positive candidate bounds the positive dimension from above only;
  • a value-preserving map mistranslates every executable action;
  • a coherent transfer hive has an incoherent derived kernel hive until it is saturated by its
    gauge, at every member;
  • equal prior-averaged models hide a necessary signal distinction;
  • a noisy sensor report must not replace the hidden state it reports;
  • an exact transfer omits the best target action;
  • a raw separator needs an invariance argument before it refutes an equivalence;
  • a complete family search and an interrupted search support different claims;
  • a missing report and a report of neither support nor opposition differ in availability;
  • closure and compression establish neither task preservation nor lower execution cost;
  • a merge that is correct on receipt sets double counts when applied to posteriors;
  • a sampled relevance screen drops a distinction that exact refinement keeps;
  • a simulation proves a safety property that no exact quotient could;


                                              71
MeTTaDinC                                                     Specification draft, 4 November 2026


  • an unmeasured correspondence is not a measured zero;
  • an item statistic of a machine with fewer queries is lifted to a store whose item machine
    has more, and a safety result is requested along a simulation whose abstract machine is
    not the request’s target: both are InvalidInput, naming the mismatch;
  • a receipt stream is edited while its declared digest stays the same: InvalidInput, returned
    before any count or interval, with no Estimated value;
  • both graded checks pass under an indiscrete code tolerance, and exact induction is Refuted
    with a literal-congruence witness (claims C01–C03);
  • a bound on trace discrepancy holds over every fixed word and is Refuted over observation-
    based policies with a policy-tree witness, while the first release answers Unsupported
    (O01–O04);
  • a behavioral distance is the supremum of test values that no finite test attains, so a
    measurement at precision zero is Unknown, while a horizon value, a bound below the
    supremum and the discounted value are decided (U01–U04).
The lifting, safety and edited-stream entries are negative acceptance fixtures of the application
bundles: the lifting and safety fixtures belong to the live-memory guide [26] and the edited
stream to the worked example of the open-item store [27]. They are part of the acceptance
suite in the same way as the fixtures of this package, and the implementation guide lists the
whole negative acceptance suite with the statuses an implementation must return.

16.4    Performance measurement
Admission, compilation, execution, certificate production and replay are measured separately,
with the relevant scaling variables: carrier size or symbolic description size, action and query
counts, transition sparsity, rational bit length, decision-diagram size, proof-log size, matrix
dimension and observer-family size. Comparisons use the same semantic scope and arithmetic;
a floating-point approximation and an exact rational computation are different workloads unless
an approximation contract relates them.

16.5    Open problems
  1. Behavioral distances for several actions. The complexity of the operator Φγ with a
     maximum over actions is not settled by the single-transition results [35]; exact algorithms
     with bit-complexity bounds, and compact coupling certificates from policy iteration
     [38, 37], remain to be worked out.
  2. Frontier algorithms. The weakness–defect frontier is NP-hard in general; approximation
     algorithms with guarantees for behavioral defects, and their relation to Kullback–Leibler
     aggregation, are open.
  3. Lower bounds for positive realizations. Certifying that no positive realization is smaller
     than a given dimension is what a quantumity gap needs [22]; certificate formats for such
     lower bounds, and their relation to nonnegative-rank bounds [53], are open.
  4. Graded transfer hives. Holonomies that are symmetries only up to the edge defects, least
     coherent refinements and gauge saturation for graded transfer libraries need a theory and
     algorithms beyond linear programming on small hives.
  5. A verified obligation generator, or translation validation strong enough to remove the
     generator from the trusted base.


                                               72
MeTTaDinC                                                          Specification draft, 4 November 2026


  6. Symbolic stochastic machines, with decision diagrams over rational terminals, and their
     certificates.
  7. Learned observers and learned predictive-state models, which enter as Estimated linear
     realizations and need audit protocols.
  8. Certificates under self-modification. Goal contracts, certified rewrites and dynamic closure
     of the goal observer [17] are interface changes in MeTTaDinC’s sense; extending the
     transport rules to rewrites of the checker itself is open.
  9. Partial identity tracking. Merges whose correspondences are only partly measured need
     an approximation theory that degrades gracefully between the shared and fresh extremes
     [11].

16.6    Closing
What the language is for, in the end, is letting a system that rewrites its own descriptions—
forking its memories, merging what its copies learned, translating skills between versions of
itself—ask of each rewrite how much it forgot and whether the forgetting will matter later, and
get an answer it can check rather than an answer it must trust. There turn out to be several
defensible answers to “what can be forgotten?”—forget what no experiment will ever reveal,
forget what no record of observations will reveal, forget what a symmetry hides—and much of
the design work here is in keeping those answers from being mistaken for one another. The
mathematics needed for that is mostly old, and the distinction calculus mostly supplies a way
of holding it together so the answers compose; the executable core is small enough to build
soon, and the questions it opens, about frontiers, signed representations and self-modifying
checkers, look large enough to be worth building it for.

   The assurance policy assigns each status on one ground, and consumers declare which results
they accept: a result is Checked only on the grounds Exact or Replayed, Refuted only on the
ground Witness, Estimated only on the ground Statistical, and Conditional when it rests on
an unreplayed backend answer, a declared assumption, an Estimated premise or a Conditional
premise, in every request and library; a value of a behavioral or trace distance is Checked only
through the registered certificate kinds (certificate_kinds), whatever algorithm computed it.

                                        model (finite enumeration, exact rational arithmetic, partition
                                        refinement, splitting trees replayed on representatives); a
                                        distance value needs, in addition, registered certificates for both
                                        ends of its interval
  Replayed                              Checked: a certificate that the core’s replay checker validates (a
                                        replayed witness, a coupling, a matching, stability equations, an

  TrustedBackend                        Conditional: the verdict rests on an external backend’s answer

    Witness types, with the dynamics they serve. Replay accepts any witness that replays; the
canonical witness, recorded in the manifest, is the least one: shortest word first, then pairs in
lexicographic order of carrier declaration, then actions and queries in declaration order, then
words in numeric order; policy trees of least depth first, then actions in declaration order at
nodes taken in order of their histories.

   Witness              Fields, dynamics and replay
   WordWitness          Fields: Pair, SameCode, Word, Query, Left, Right. Dynamics: Deterministic.
   ModalTestWitness     Fields: Pair, Formula (a graph with shared subformulas), Left, Right. Dynamics:
                        Stochastic, Nondeterministic.
   TraceEventWitness    Fields: Pair, Word, Event, Left, Right. Dynamics: Stochastic. A trace refutation
                        over the policy class Words: one action word, one event on observation sequences,
                        and its two exact probabilities.
   PolicyTreeWitness    Fields: Pair, Tree, Event, Left, Right. Dynamics: Stochastic. A trace refutation
                        over the policy class ObservationBased. A finite tree of nodes keyed by observed
                        histories (y0 a1 y1 ... ak yk), k below the horizon; the root is keyed by the initial
                        observation and the children of a node by its action and the next observation;
                        each node names an action, or a rational law on actions. The tree reads observed
                        histories only: replay keys every node by its history, runs both states through the
                        same tree, and requires a node for every history of positive probability from
                        either state. Exact propagation of both states through the tree gives the two
                        joint laws of actions and observations; Left and Right are the probabilities of
                        Event, a set of complete histories, and the witness certifies a discrepancy of at
                        least |Left - Right|.
   UpdateWitness        Fields: Pair, Action, Codes or Class, Left, Right. Dynamics: Deterministic,
                        Stochastic, Nondeterministic.
   CongruenceWitness    Fields: Pair, SameCode, Action, Left, Right. Dynamics: Deterministic,
                        Stochastic, Nondeterministic. A failure of literal congruence in InduceExact: two
                        states with the same code value whose successors under Action have different
                        code values (deterministic), different laws on code values (stochastic: a code value
                        and its two masses) or different sets of code values (nondeterministic); it refutes
                        exact induction even when the code tolerance identifies the successor codes.
   ObligationWitness    Fields: Obligation, Word or State. Dynamics: Deterministic, Stochastic,
                        Nondeterministic.


    Certificates for graded distances. A distance value enters a result only through these kinds,
whatever algorithm computed it; a measurement with Precision 0 is Checked only when a lower
and an upper certificate meet; at discount 1 over all finite words no lower certificate other than
tests is registered, so a Precision 0 measurement whose tests do not reach the upper certificate
is Unknown with the certified interval.

  Certificate          Bound, data, replay and scope
  Test                 Lower bound. Data: a test (word, modal, trace-event or policy-tree test) with its
                       two exact values. Replay: evaluate the test at both states exactly. Scope: word
                       and modal tests bound the distance at their own discount; trace-event and
                       policy-tree tests are undiscounted probabilities of an event and bound the
                       distance at discount 1 only; a test of depth h bounds the horizon-h distance; no
                       finite test need attain the distance over all finite words.
  Prefixpoint          Upper bound. Data: a kernel D with one coupling (stochastic) or two matchings
                       (nondeterministic) per pair and action. Replay: check the marginals or matching
                       domains and D >= max(d0, gamma * E_theta D) for every pair and action.
                       Scope: every horizon.
  LayeredPrefixpoint   Upper bound. Data: kernels D_0 .. D_h with couplings or matchings for every
                       layer, pair and action. Replay: check D_0 >= d0 and D_(t+1) >= max(d0,
                       gamma * E_theta_t D_t) for every layer, pair and action. Scope: horizon h.
  DualPostfixpoint     Lower bound. Data: a kernel D of a stochastic machine; for every pair with D
                       above d0 an action and rational dual potentials f, g on the supports of its two
                       successor laws. Replay: check f(u) - g(v) <= D(u,v) on the supports and D(x,x’)
                       <= gamma * (E f - E g) for those pairs, D <= d0 elsewhere. Scope: stochastic
                       machines at discount below 1, where Phi_gamma contracts; at discount 1 the
                       same data bound only some fixed point of the distance operator, not necessarily
                       the least.
