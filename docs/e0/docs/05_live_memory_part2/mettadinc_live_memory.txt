                                                Abstract
      Part I of this guide stated Omega’s memory and goal decisions as representations of finite
      machines and checked them exhaustively. The live system is not finite: items carry real
      timestamps, evidence arrives from an open set of sources, confidence and deadline pressure
      are real numbers, and the stores grow without bound. This part extends the patterns to
      that system. Its central object is a summary statistic written as a certificate: an encoding
      of the live state together with an abstract update for every operation and a read map
      for every query. When the update and read identities hold for every state, the statistic
      answers every declared query after every sequence of operations, so a store can keep the
      statistic in place of the history. The identities are first-order formulas over the theories of
      the carriers, so a solver decides them for all states at once, whether the states hold real
      ages, real confidences or finite sets of identifiers of any size; until the solver’s proofs are
      checked, such a verdict is reported as Conditional on the solver. Five constructions produce
      such statistics: abstraction to a finite model, which carries Part I’s exhaustive verdicts over
      to real values along a stuttering simulation; saturation at the largest threshold a query
      reads; lifting from items to keyed stores and histograms; identity-bounded sets, which count
      distinct sources up to a cap and merge exactly across forks; and approximate or one-sided
      statistics with explicit error contracts. What declarations cannot settle, such as how often
      the running system misses a consumer or suggests a stale item, is estimated from logged
      receipts with confidence sequences and e-process monitors, which remain valid however often
      they are consulted, and whose one-sided claims hold without assuming that the receipts
      share one rate. Every pattern of Part I is worked again on live values. A recall window
      remains an inadequate source of item status at real time resolution, with witnesses found by
      the solver. The event statistic of an open item and its saturated form are exact for stores of
      any size. The memory that serves a three-day follow-up goal must keep ages the two-day
      goal lets it forget. Part I’s verdicts on two arbitration rules hold for real confidence and
      pressure. Under the floor design of the MetaMo-shaped dynamics, every run that starts at
      a real state inside the safe region stays inside it, while swapping appraisal and decision can
      move a goal weight by as much as 9/10. At a stale-suggestion rate of one in ten, a monitor
      tuned to one in five raised its alarm in about half of the simulated runs, and monitors that
      average over alternative rates raised it in every run.

1     Introduction
Part I of this guide built its patterns small on purpose. An open item’s age was a whole number
of days up to seven, with one more value for anything older, evidence came from three sources,
and motivational state lived on a grid of quarters and halves. On models of that size every
request could be answered by enumeration, and every refutation came with the least witness.
Part I’s section on limits named what this left open [1, Section 14]: claims about an open-ended
store need “a bounded interface, a symbolic sufficient statistic, or an approximation contract”
[2, Section 12.4], and MetaMo’s principles had been checked only on finite instances.
    The live system has none of that finiteness. In WMTM (Working Medium-Term Memory),
the memory and reasoning layer of the ProtoCosmo2 agent, open items carry timestamps, a
reply can arrive at any moment, and the journal grows by the day [4]. Evidence for a claim
can come from any number of sources, and the same source can report twice. The plan that
reimplements WMTM around the Omega Core Ontology, version 2 (OCO/2), keeps attention
values with decay anchors [5], and the claims handled by GOLEM-Iter, the framework under
which proposed changes to the agent are tested and promoted, carry confidences and deadlines
that vary continuously [6]. Exact claims about such a system have to hold on infinite state
spaces. The running system also depends on components that no declaration describes, such as
the language model, the semantic parser and the detector that decides whether a message is a
reply, and some of its properties can only be measured.
    For the exact claims this part uses summary statistics. A summary statistic is a compact
encoding of the live state, together with an abstract update for every operation and a read map
for every query, such that two identities hold: reading the encoding answers each query, and
encoding after an operation equals updating the encoding. Those two identities are enough to
make the encoding answer every query after every sequence of operations, so a store can keep
the statistic and drop the history. In the terms of the distinction calculus, a memory embodies
a decision about which distinctions to keep, and a summary statistic states that decision in
a compact form whose defining identities a solver can check for all states at once. For the
measured properties this part uses estimation. A store that keeps a statistic can log a receipt
each time a consumer is or is not served, and the rate of misses is then a quantity to estimate
from the receipts. The estimates used here are confidence sequences and e-processes, which
remain valid however often they are consulted, as a monitor on a live system must be.

1.2    Status
This is a design with validated fixtures, like Part I. The six modules of this part conform to
the registry together with the patches of the worked examples, of Part I and of this part. The
verdict of every symbolic request in the modules has been obtained twice: once by the Z3 solver

2     Background
2.1    Part I in brief
A MeTTaDinC machine has a carrier of states, actions that change the state, and observers,
the queries its consumers ask. A representation encodes each state as a code. The specification
separates three obligations for a representation [2, Section 4.2]: two states with the same
code answer every query alike (now), have successors with the same code under every action
(updates), and answer alike after every sequence of actions (behavior). The specification calls
them current adequacy, update compatibility and behavioral adequacy, with defects δnow , δupd
and δbeh that are zero exactly when the obligations hold. Part I attached a layer of such
declarations to Omega’s memory and goal machinery: consumers declared with their custody, a
background checker, a certificate store keyed by interface digests, receipts on context blocks
and goal suggestions, and a small interface the agent can use to ask about its own memory [1].
A result is Checked, Refuted with a replayable witness, Unknown when a budget ran out, or
Estimated when it is a statistical claim about data; Part I’s verdicts are exact enumerations
of finite models, so they are Checked. The specification has one more status, Conditional, for
a derivation that holds under named assumptions not yet discharged [2, Section 2.2]. The
symbolic verdicts of this part take it, for the reason Section 7.2 gives.
    The eight patterns of Part I, which this part revisits, are: closing open items and the goal
engine’s age cap and deduplication (Pattern 1); choosing the context block from working memory
(Pattern 2); consolidating evidence without losing contradictions, evidence lineage or an anchor’s
cues (Pattern 3); task checkpoints and read sets (Pattern 4); goals as observers on memory, and
sorting proposed goal changes (Pattern 5); conflicts between goals and a prohibition (Pattern 6);
MetaMo’s principles on an implemented motivational dynamics (Pattern 7); and a monitor on
the goal engine’s stale suggestions (Pattern 8).

2.2    Sufficient statistics
Fisher introduced sufficiency in 1922: a statistic of a sample is sufficient for a parameter when,
once its value is known, the rest of the sample carries no further information about the parameter
[11]. The sample mean and the sample size are sufficient for the mean of a normal distribution
with known variance; the number of successes is sufficient for the rate of a coin. Sufficiency is
what lets a running sum stand in for a list of numbers. It is also rare in a precise sense: under
regularity conditions, a sufficient statistic whose size does not grow with the sample exists only
for exponential families of distributions, which is the content of the Pitman–Koopman–Darmois
theorem [12].
     The statistics of this part are sufficient in a different sense, which is why small ones are
common. Sufficiency is asked relative to a declared set of queries and a declared set of operations,
not relative to everything the data might say. A message store that only ever needs to answer
“is this item still open and at most seven days old” does not have to remember the text of replies;
it has to remember whether one arrived and, for open items, how old they are. Automata theory
has the same idea in its Myhill–Nerode theorem: the states of the smallest automaton for a
language are the classes of strings that no continuation can tell apart [13]. A summary statistic
is a machine-level version of that construction, with queries in place of acceptance.

2.3    Summaries that forget on purpose
Data-structure research has long built summaries that keep only what a query needs, often
trading exactness for size. A Bloom filter stores a set in an array of bits by setting k hashed
positions per element; it can answer “maybe in the set” for an element that is absent, but never
“absent” for an element that is present [14]. A Count–Min sketch estimates frequencies from
a small array of counters and overestimates by a bounded amount with a stated probability
[15]; HyperLogLog estimates the number of distinct elements from an array of small registers, a
few thousand in typical configurations [16]; exponential histograms count events over a sliding
window to a stated relative error [17]. Two properties of such summaries recur below. Some
are one-sided, never wrong in one direction, which is what a safety decision needs. Some
are mergeable: the summaries of two data sets combine into the summary of their union [18].
Mergeability is the property that conflict-free replicated data types turn into a design principle
for replicas that exchange state in any order [19].

2.4    Deciding claims about infinitely many states
A claim such as “for every item of every age and every waiting time, encoding after the wait
equals updating the encoding” quantifies over infinitely many states, so it cannot be checked by
enumeration. Abstract interpretation is one classical way to reason about such claims: replace
sets of concrete states by abstract values and compute with those [20]. The other way, used
here for the identities themselves, is to state the negation of the claim as a formula and ask a
satisfiability-modulo-theories (SMT) solver for a model of it. Z3 and cvc5 are two such solvers
[21, 22]. If the solver reports the negation unsatisfiable, the claim holds for every state; if it
returns a model, the model is a concrete counterexample, which can be replayed by evaluating
the declarations at the values the model gives.
    What a solver can decide depends on the theory of the carriers. Linear arithmetic over the
reals is decidable and fast. Arithmetic with products of variables is decidable over the reals, by
the classical results of Tarski and the cylindrical algebraic decomposition of Collins [23, 24],
but costlier; the obligations of this part that involve an evidence update, which multiplies the
confidence by the size of the increment, need it. Finite sets of elements of an uninterpreted
sort, with insertion, union and cardinality, have a decision procedure, developed in CVC4 and

available in its successor cvc5 [25]; because the elements are uninterpreted, a verdict holds for
sets of identifiers of any size. Finally, a solver’s “unsat” is only as trustworthy as the solver,

library, as unsatisfiability proofs checked by a small independent checker [2, Section 15.3], and

2.5   Anytime-valid inference
A monitor on a live system looks at its data again and again. Classical confidence intervals
and tests assume that the sample size was fixed in advance; checking a fixed-sample test after
every new observation and stopping at the first rejection inflates the error rate, a problem
documented in clinical trials decades ago [27]. Anytime-valid procedures remove the problem.
    A confidence sequence is a sequence of intervals that contains the true value at every time
simultaneously with probability at least 1 − α, so it can be read after every observation and
acted on at any moment [28, 29, 30]. An e-process is a nonnegative process whose expected
value at any stopping time is at most one when a null hypothesis holds; a likelihood ratio built
up one observation at a time is the simplest example. Ville’s inequality says that a nonnegative
supermartingale that starts at one reaches 1/α with probability at most α [31], so an alarm at
1/α has false-alarm probability at most α however long the monitor runs [32]. Both tools fit
receipts: each receipt is one more observation, and the claim the agent reports is valid at the
moment it is reported.


3     Summary statistics as certificates
3.1   Machines on real carriers
The machines of this part are MeTTaDinC machines whose carriers are not enumerated. A
carrier is declared by a type and, where needed, a predicate: the items of Pattern 1 are the
triples (a, c, e) of an age a ≥ 0 in days, a flag c that says whether a closing reply has been seen,
and the time e since that reply, with e ≤ a. In the module this is (Carrier Item (Of (Tuple
Rational Bool Rational)) (Subset Raw well-formed)). Actions come in families indexed
by parameters: waiting d days, for every d ≥ 0, is one action family Twait : X × Q≥0 → X,
declared as (Action (wait $d) (wait $d) (For $d Days)). Queries are maps oq : X → Vq .
A machine may also have merge entries m : X × X → X, which combine the states of two forks
of one memory. A word is a finite sequence of actions with their parameters, and Tw is their
composition.

3.2   Definition and sufficiency
Definition 3.1 (Summary statistic). A summary statistic of a machine M with carrier X
consists of a set S of values, an encoding s : X → S, an abstract update stepa : S × Pa → S for
every action family a with parameter set Pa , a read map readq : S → Vq for every query q, and
an abstract merge mstepm : S × S → S for every merge entry m, such that for all x, y ∈ X and
p ∈ Pa :
      (C) Ta (x, p) ∈ X and m(x, y) ∈ X;             (R) oq (x) = readq (s(x));
      (U) s(Ta (x, p)) = stepa (s(x), p);            (M) s(m(x, y)) = mstepm (s(x), s(y)).

Proposition 3.2 (Sufficiency). For a summary statistic and every history h, s(JhK) = JhKS .
In particular oq (Tw x) = readq (stepw (s(x))) for every word w and query q, and two states with

In plain terms. If reading the summary answers each question and updating the summary
keeps pace with every operation, then the summary answers every question after any history,
and the store needs nothing else.

Remark 3.3 (Relation to the specification). Condition (R) says that the current defect δnow
of the encoding is zero, with decoder readq , and (U) that the update defect δupd is zero, with
abstract update stepa ; Proposition 4.5 of the specification says that such maps exist when
the defects vanish and that together they make the behavioral defect zero [2, Section 4.2]. A
summary statistic is that proposition with the maps supplied. On an enumerated carrier the
difference is small. On a symbolic carrier it changes the shape of the obligations: the defect
form compares pairs of states with equal codes and asks whether some function exists, while
the certificate form is one identity per action and per query over a single state, a universally
quantified formula that a decision procedure handles directly. The supplied maps are also
an implementation: the store keeps s(x) and applies stepa . The cost-aware CoDD paper’s
implemented concept packages an encoder, update procedures, consumer queries, guards, laws,
certificates and resource records [10, Definition 6.1]; a summary statistic is the part of such a
package that a checker can establish exactly.

3.3   The coarsest statistic
A context is a history with one hole. Two states are behaviorally equivalent, x ≈ y, when every
context C and query q give oq (JC[x]K) = oq (JC[y]K). Without merges, contexts are words, and
this is the relation of Part I’s MinimizeForQueries.

Proposition 3.4 (The coarsest statistic).
  (i) Every summary statistic refines ≈: s(x) = s(y) implies x ≈ y.
 (ii) The quotient map x 7→ [x]≈ is a summary statistic.
(iii) A summary statistic is coarsest, that is s(x) = s(y) exactly when x ≈ y, if and only if any
      two states with different values are separated by some context and query. The coarsest
      statistic is unique up to renaming its values.

Proof. (i) is Proposition 3.2 applied inside contexts. For (ii), x ≈ y implies Ta (x, p) ≈ Ta (y, p),
m(x, z) ≈ m(y, z) and m(z, x) ≈ m(z, y), since a context of the result is a context of the
argument, and implies oq (x) = oq (y) through the empty context; so updates, merges and reads
are well defined on classes. (iii) follows from (i) and (ii).

    On a finite machine the coarsest statistic is computed by partition refinement, which is the
content of Part I’s MinimizeForQueries and of Theorem 4.2 of the goal-preservation paper [9].
On an infinite carrier there is no general algorithm. The approach here is to propose a statistic,
check (C), (R), (U) and (M) with a solver, and establish minimality by giving a separating
context as a function of the two values, whose correctness is again a solver obligation. In the
engine statistic of Pattern 1, for example, two open items with ages v < v ′ ≤ 7 are separated by
waiting 7 − v ′ + (v ′ − v)/2 days, after which the first is still within the seven-day cap and the
second is past it.
     The coarsest statistic is the smallest exact memory, and it need not be the cheapest to
maintain. The cost-aware CoDD paper observes that among sufficient encodings one with more
values, or with redundancy, may have much cheaper operations, since the quotient does not order
its implementations by runtime or memory [10, Section 6.2]. Its example also shows the update
language deciding what suffices: under insertions and deletions the bit “some object remains”
cannot be updated exactly, and recomputing it means scanning the whole population, while
an incremental count, which keeps more distinctions, is updated in constant time. Section 4.4
meets the same effect of deletions with sources of evidence.

4      Five constructions
The five constructions below cover every pattern of this part. Each comes with a proposition
that says when it is exact, and the last one says how to bound the error of statistics that are
not.

4.1     Abstraction to a finite model
The first construction reuses Part I. Many live states differ only in real values that the consumers
read through thresholds: a claim’s confidence enters the goals only through whether it is at
least 4/5, deadline pressure only through whether it is at least 1/2. A map h that sends each
live state to its classes lands in a finite model, and if the actions respect the classes, Part I’s
exhaustive verdicts about the finite model carry over.

Proposition 4.1 (Abstraction to a finite model). Let M be a machine with carrier X, N a
machine with carrier Y , and h : X → Y a map such that every query q of M is a query of N
with oq = o′q ◦ h, and every action family a of M has a counterpart ā in N .
    (i) (Lockstep.) Suppose h(Ta (x, p)) = Tā′ (h(x)) for all x and p, and that every merge entry
        m of M has a counterpart m′ in N with h(m(x, y)) = m′ (h(x), h(y)). Then h, with
        stepa = Tā′ , mstepm = m′ and readq = o′q , is a summary statistic of M . Hence oq (Tw x) =
        o′q (Tw̄′ h(x)) for every word w, where w̄ replaces each action of w by its counterpart, so

MeTTaDinC                                           Omega memory and goals, Part II, 4 November 2026


      every claim about N ’s answers after words of counterpart actions from h(x) holds for M
      after the corresponding words from x. Claims about words of N that contain other actions
      do not transfer.
 (ii) (Stuttering.) If h(Ta (x, p)) ∈ {h(x), Tā′ (h(x))} for all x and p, then for every run
      x0 , x1 , . . . , xn of M the sequence h(x0 ), . . . , h(xn ) with consecutive repetitions removed is a
      run of N . Every state reachable from x0 maps to a state reachable from h(x0 ), and each of
      the three safety forms of the specification’s Simulation library that holds in N from h(x0 )
      holds in M from x0 . The forms are Shows (no reachable state shows a given answer),
      EnterFrom (no transition enters a state showing one answer from a state showing neither
      it nor a second one) and ReachWithin (no run of at most k steps reaches a state showing
      a given answer) [2, Section 8.2].

Proof. (i) is Definition 3.1 with the maps named, and the identity for words is Proposition 3.2.
For (ii), each step of the run either leaves the image fixed or moves it by a step of N ; removing
the fixed steps leaves a run of N , so reachable states map to reachable states, and Shows
transfers because related states answer alike. A step of M that leaves the image fixed changes
no query answer, so it cannot be a transition that EnterFrom forbids; any other step is matched
by a step of N with the same answers at both ends. A run of M is never shorter than its image,
which gives ReachWithin.

    Stuttering is the natural relation for real-valued state. A small increment of evidence leaves
a claim’s class unchanged; a larger one moves it across the threshold, which is a step of the
finite model. The converse transfer, from a violation in N to one in M , holds only when the
abstract run can be realized by concrete parameters. The checker tries to realize it one step
at a time, asking the solver for a parameter that moves the live state into the next abstract
state; a realized run is a Refuted witness about M , and an abstract run that cannot be realized
gives Unknown. The specification’s counterexample-guided loop also returns Unknown for a
spurious abstract run, together with a predicate that refines the abstraction [2, Section 8.2];
the checker of this part stops at Unknown and leaves the refinement step open. Pattern 6 uses
both directions.

4.2   Saturation at thresholds
The second construction handles clocks. An item’s age grows with every wait; queries compare
it with thresholds, such as the goal engine’s seven-day cap or a two-day follow-up window. An
age beyond the largest threshold can never change an answer again, because ages only grow, so
the statistic can replace it by a marker.

Proposition 4.2 (Saturation). Let a machine have a clock component a ∈ Q≥0 that every wait
d ≥ 0 advances to a + d, that other actions leave unchanged, and that the queries read only
through comparisons a ≤ θi with thresholds θ1 < · · · < θk , together with components the clock
does not affect. Write θ = θk .
  (i) The map satθ (a) = (a > θ, a · [a ≤ θ]), with the abstract wait that sends (t, v) to
      (t ∨ v + d > θ, (v + d) · [¬t ∧ v + d ≤ θ]), together with the other components unchanged,
      is a summary statistic.
 (ii) If the query at θ is read on a set of live items that waits do not leave, then two live ages
      v < v ′ ≤ θ are separated by waiting θ − v ′ + (v ′ − v)/2, and an age at most θ is separated
      from the marker immediately; on live items the statistic is the coarsest that keeps the
      other components.

(iii) Adding a query with a threshold θ′ > θ makes satθ inadequate: a live age in (θ, θ′ ] and
      a live age above θ′ share the value (true, 0) and answer the new query differently. The
      coarsest statistic then saturates at θ′ .

Proof. (i) If a > θ then a + d > θ, and if a ≤ θ the new value is decided by a + d, so the abstract
wait commutes with encoding; each comparison a ≤ θi is read as ¬t ∧ v ≤ θi . (ii) After the wait
the first age is θ − (v ′ − v)/2 ≤ θ and the second θ + (v ′ − v)/2 > θ. (iii) is immediate.

In plain terms. Remember an age only until the last deadline any goal can still read; once a
goal with a later deadline is added, the memory must keep ages that long.
    The rule in (iii) is the live form of a transport rule of the specification: adding a query
resumes refinement from the current memory and can only make it finer, and removing one lets
it coarsen [2, Section 4.8]. When a flag makes every clock value equivalent, as a closed item
is neither suggested nor open whatever its age, the coarsest statistic also forgets the clock for
those items; the statistics of Patterns 1 and 5 do this by resetting the value of a closed item to
a fixed one. If, moreover, a flagged item and an item past the last threshold answer every query
alike from then on, the coarsest statistic merges them too. The engine statistic of Pattern 1
does; the statistic of Pattern 5 keeps them apart because it keeps the open flag.

4.4     Identity-bounded sets
The fourth construction counts distinct identifiers from an open set. Part I’s evidence consumers
ask whether any contradicting source has reported, how many distinct supporting sources there
are up to two, and whether at least two distinct contradicting sources have reported. On three
sources the coarsest memory was, for each polarity, “no source, the only source so far, or two or
more”. With an unbounded set of sources the same memory is the identity-bounded set.

Definition 4.4 (DK ). For a positive integer K and a finite set A of identifiers, DK (A) = A if
|A| < K and DK (A) = Many otherwise. On values, insertion, union and size are

      CapInsertK (x, A) = DK (A ∪ {x}),      CapInsertK (x, Many) = Many,
      CapUnionK (A, B) = DK (A ∪ B),             CapUnionK (c, d) = Many if c or d is Many,
          CapSizeK (A) = |A|,                    CapSizeK (Many) = K.

  (i) The identities

        DK (A ∪ {x}) = CapInsertK (x, DK (A)),       DK (A ∪ B) = CapUnionK (DK (A), DK (B)),

 (ii) Under insertions it is the coarsest such statistic: two different values are separated by
      inserting at most one identifier.
(iii) CapUnionK is commutative, associative and idempotent, so replicas that exchange values

4.5    Approximate and one-sided statistics
The fifth construction is for statistics that are not exact, either because the implementation
rounds or because an exact statistic would be too large. Two kinds of claims remain available:
a bound on the error, and a guarantee that the error has one sign.

5     Claims about the running system
A summary statistic is sufficient relative to its input events. If the detector that decides whether
a message is a reply misses one, the open-item statistic faithfully keeps a settled item open; if the
parser drops the source of a judgment, the evidence statistic never sees it. What the language
model does with a context block is outside every declaration as well. These are quantities of
the running system, and they are rates. The goal-preservation paper draws the line in the
same place: “Exact program proofs and statistical response audits solve different parts of the
problem. A program proof can establish which states and transitions are possible; sampling can
estimate response probabilities in those states” [9, Section 6].

5.1   What an Estimated claim states
The specification asks an Estimated result to name its estimand, its estimator, the sampling
protocol, the sample size, an error bound with its failure probability, and the rule by which
several estimates are combined [2, Section 7.4]. It also names the population the receipts sample,
the rule that selects which units become rows and when, and the version of the policy under
which the rows were produced, because a rate measured under one policy says nothing about
another until an argument carries it over. The streams of this part’s modules declare these
three fields, and EstimateRate reports them with its interval. For the claims of this part the
raw material is a receipt: a record, written when a context block is built or a suggestion is
made, of which consumers were served. Part I proposed the receipt formats [1, Appendix C]. A
column of receipts is a sequence of binary events X1 , X2 , . . ., such as “the anchor consumer was
missed in snapshot i”, read in an order fixed before their labels are known. Write µi for the
conditional probability that Xi = 1 given the events before it.
    Two working assumptions appear below, and each result states which one it uses. The
stronger is that every µi equals one rate p, the probability of the event per snapshot under the
current workload; it is needed when an interval is read as an estimate of p. The weaker assumes
nothing about the µi . A claim that the rate is below, or above, a fixed target is then a claim
about their running average, and Proposition 5.1 shows that the procedure below supports it
with the same guarantee.

5.4   Comparing two policies on the same snapshots
Two selection policies can be run on the same snapshots, one live and one in shadow, so their
receipts come in pairs. Snapshots on which both policies serve a consumer, or both miss it, say
nothing about which is better. On the discordant snapshots, where exactly one misses, let b
count those where the first misses and c those where the second does. If the first policy misses
with probability p1 and the second with p2 per snapshot, then among discordant snapshots the
first misses with probability r, and p1 − p2 has the sign of r − 1/2. The confidence sequence of
Section 5.2, applied to the b + c discordant snapshots, gives an interval for r; an interval above
one half says the first policy misses more often. This is the sequential form of McNemar’s test
[34], and the proposed request CompareRates computes it. Read as a one-sided claim against
one half, it needs no common rate either: Proposition 5.1 applies to the discordant snapshots in
the order they occur.

Each pattern of Part I is worked again below. Every module of this section except the receipts

6.2   Pattern 2: context blocks under a token budget
The problem. Part I compared selection policies on working memories of three to five slots
and found the consumer-aware policy the only one that served the reply writer, the speaker’s
open items and an anchor’s cues once the block had room for one item per consumer. The
live working memory has sixty items with token costs between 15 and 120, the budget is 1,500
tokens, and items are chosen by first fit in the policy’s order. Part I noted that with costed
items the consumer-aware policy becomes a small knapsack over the consumers’ required items
[1, Section 5.4]. The guarantee can be stated for any number of items.

Proposition 6.1 (Consumer-aware blocks). For each consumer let its required item be the
most recent item of its kind, if there is one, and let cR be the total token cost of the required
items. If cR ≤ B, the block built by first fit from the order that puts the required items first


                                                22
MeTTaDinC                                          Omega memory and goals, Part II, 4 November 2026


serves every consumer, whatever the number of items, their costs and their scores. If cR > B,
no block within the budget serves every consumer.

Proof. First fit takes the required items first, and the running total never exceeds cR ≤ B. A
block that serves every consumer contains every required item, so its cost is at least cR .

    What the block builder has to maintain is, for each consumer, the identity and cost of its
required item. Under arrivals this is a statistic: a new item of a kind replaces the old one.
Under removals the builder needs the next most recent item of the kind as well, so it keeps a
recency list per kind.

use case repairs regenerative depth by keeping “a small, diverse, provenance-bearing set of
integrity incidents outside lossy compression” [6]; for consumers that read counts of distinct
sources up to K, DK is that set, with K the largest count any consumer reads. Two cautions
follow from Proposition 4.5. If sources can be withdrawn, DK cannot be maintained, and claims
subject to retraction must keep their full set of distinct sources. And DK counts identifiers,
so aliases of one source must be resolved before insertion; how often they are not is a rate for
receipts.

6.4   Pattern 4: read sets at scale
The problem. Part I showed that task checkpoints without read sets are adequate until a
withdrawal: after an evidence item is withdrawn, a restart must know which results read it.
The OCO/2 plan’s TaskCheckpoint records a read set for this purpose [5]. With thousands of
results and a large evidence store, the question becomes how to answer “which current results
read the withdrawn item” quickly.

Two statistics. The inverted index maps each evidence item to the results that read it. It is
a reorganization of the read sets, maintained when a result is created or retired, and it answers
the question exactly; the claim to check is conformance between index and read sets, by the
replay of Section 5.3. A Bloom filter per result over its read set is one-sided in the sense of
Proposition 4.7: it never misses a result that read the item, and it sometimes flags one that did
not.

Results. With 2,000 results whose read sets hold 5 to 50 of 100,000 evidence items, 500
random withdrawals made 239 results stale. The index found exactly those. Filters of 10 bits
per item with 7 hash functions missed none. They raised 8,483 false alarms in the 999,744
pairs of a withdrawal and a result that had not read the withdrawn item, a rate of 0.85 percent
against 0.82 percent predicted by the formula for ideal hash functions. Over the 500 withdrawals,
though, 1,720 of the 1,761 results that never became stale were flagged at least once, so 1,959
of the 2,000 results were flagged in all.

Notes for implementers. The index needs memory in proportion to the total size of the
read sets and an update whenever a result is created or retired; the filters need about ten
bits per read and no shared structure, and cost extra revalidations: about 0.85 percent of
the unaffected results for each withdrawal handled on its own. A sweep that handles many
withdrawals at once gains little from them, since over 500 withdrawals nearly every result was
flagged at least once. A restart that has to be trusted should consult the index. The filters suit
withdrawals handled one at a time when the index is unavailable. In either case the absence of
false negatives is the property a safety argument relies on, and only the filters’ false-positive
rate has to be estimated.

7     The language additions
The modules of this part use the forms of the worked examples and of Part I, and propose the
additions below, collected in ls_registry_patch.json and listed in full in Appendix B. With
all patches applied the static checker reports the six modules of this part as conforming to the
registry, together with the ten modules of Part I’s module file, which the checker reads because
LiveArbitration imports two machines from one of them.

7.1   Libraries and declarations
RationalArith supplies order, Min and Max on Rational. Its obligations are decided by linear
arithmetic when every product has a constant factor and by nonlinear arithmetic otherwise,
with refutations reported as Section 3.4 describes.
   BoundedSets adds the types (FiniteSet T) and (Capped T), the operations Insert, Union
and Size, and the identity-bounded operations Cap, CapInsert, CapUnion and CapSize of
Section 4.4, with the marker Many. Obligations are decided in the theory of finite sets with
cardinality over an uninterpreted element sort.
     Statistics adds three declarations. A Statistic names a machine, an encoding, a Step for
each action entry, a Read for each query and a MergeStep for each merge entry, and a declaration
that lacks one is invalid input; it declares a representation, so CheckNow, CheckUpdates and
CheckBehavioral also apply to it. A Store is the machine of finite maps from keys to items of
a declared item machine, with the actions named in Global applied to every item. A Keyed
statistic lifts an item statistic to a store; its item statistic must be a statistic of the store’s
item machine, unless an optional View field names a view of that machine, in which case the
lifted result is bound to the view’s queries (Section 4.3). The request CheckStatistic checks a
statistic by the methods Symbolic, Exhaustive (for enumerated carriers) or Lift (for keyed

MeTTaDinC                                       Omega memory and goals, Part II, 4 November 2026


statistics), and checks the premise of the lifting proposition before it lifts anything.
    Estimates adds EstimateRate and CompareRates, whose results are Estimated and name
the stream, its digest, its population, selection rule and policy, the procedure and the assumptions
of Section 5. A stream whose file no longer matches its declared digest makes both requests
InvalidInput before any count is computed. The patch adds the three optional fields Population,
Selection and Policy to the Stream declaration of the worked examples.
    Two smaller changes complete the patch. A For clause may range over a carrier that is not
enumerated when the module’s carriers are symbolic, which makes the entry one parameterized
action over which every obligation quantifies. And the Simulation declaration gains a Matching
field whose value Stutter gives the stuttering simulations of Proposition 4.1.

7.2   Back ends and certificates
The obligations of the modules fall into four theories: linear real arithmetic with Booleans and
enumerated symbols, which covers all of Patterns 1, 5 and 7 and most of Pattern 6; nonlinear
real arithmetic, for the four obligations of Pattern 6 that involve the evidence update, two
in each simulation; finite sets with cardinality over an uninterpreted sort, for Pattern 3; and
quantified formulas over arrays, which only the hand-written Z3 encoding of Pattern 3 uses.
Refutations need no trust in a solver, because their witnesses are replayed by evaluating the
declarations. Verdicts of unsatisfiability do. The specification asks that symbolic certificates
replay without the producing library [2, Section 15.3], and for linear arithmetic with equality
this is available: cvc5 emits proofs in the Alethe format for those theories, and Carcara checks
them [26]. For nonlinear arithmetic and set cardinality, cvc5’s Alethe output does not yet apply.
    The specification gives the gap one operational answer, which this part follows [2, Section 2.2].
A symbolic verdict whose proof has been checked by the core’s checker is Checked. A verdict
that rests on a solver’s answer without a checked proof is Conditional, with the assumption
written (TrustedBackend cvc5 1.4.2 ALL sha256:...): the solver, its version, the logic it
was run in and the digest of the asserted obligations. Checking the proof later is the separate
step that discharges the assumption [2, Section 2.7]. The reference evaluator applies the rule to
every request it answers, so the tables of Section 6 report the symbolic verdicts as Conditional on
cvc5. What the bundle adds to each of them is the agreement of Z3 on an independent encoding,
recorded beside the assumption under agreed_by in the oracle’s record of the verdicts; that is
evidence, and it discharges nothing. A result derived from Conditional premises carries their
assumptions: the lifted indexes of Pattern 1 and the safety of the norm-first rule in Pattern 6
are Conditional with the assumptions of the statistic and the simulation they rest on. The
Refuted verdicts are unaffected, and verdicts computed in exact arithmetic without a solver,
such as CheckMonitor, are Checked. The verdicts in linear arithmetic, which include all of
Patterns 1, 5 and 7, are the candidates for Checked status, since cvc5 can emit Alethe proofs
for that fragment and Carcara can check them.
    A consumer states which of these it acts on. Part I’s consumer declarations accept Checked
results, and a consumer may also accept results Conditional on a named backend, with a
declaration such as (Accepts Checked (Conditional (TrustedBackend cvc5))); consumers
with the frame’s custody accept only Checked results [1, Section 3.5]. A gate compares each
result’s status and assumptions with the touched consumer’s declaration, and a Conditional
result that the consumer does not accept counts as missing coverage.
    Exact consumers act only on Checked results, so a deployment that needs a nonlinear
or set-theoretic obligation for an exact consumer has two further options. It can reduce the
obligation to a covered theory: the evidence obligations of Pattern 6 become linear when the
product λ(1 − c) is replaced by a fresh variable t with 0 ≤ t ≤ 1 − c, a range that 0 ≤ λ ≤ 1
guarantees by a one-line argument, and the bundle checks the linear forms. Or it can establish


                                                 35
MeTTaDinC                                       Omega memory and goals, Part II, 4 November 2026


the obligation by a proof written once and kept with the declaration, as Proposition 4.5 is for
DK .
    These forms extend the symbolic path beyond the specification’s first release [2, Section 16.1].
Its capability manifest in the registry admits only bit-vector primitives on symbolic carriers,
with two backends: SAT with proof logs checked by an independent checker, and linear algebra
over F2 for maps that are linear there.


8     Engineering in Omega
8.1   Where the statistics live
In the live WMTM system, the goal engine reads open items from explicit markers in notes,
such as “open question for Ben:” and “decided:” [4]. The event statistic of Pattern 1 is the
pair of timestamps of the opening marker and of the first detected closing marker; the engine’s
seven-day cap is the saturation of Proposition 4.2. Part I’s deduplication by person and topic
becomes a keyed statistic over the question’s identity.
    In the OCO/2 records, goal contracts state their thresholds, which by Pattern 5 decide
where a compact statistic may saturate. The read set of a TaskCheckpoint feeds the inverted
index of Pattern 4. A WorkspaceSnapshot receipt records the served consumers, the cost of
the required items and the budget, as Pattern 2 asks. Admission records of evidence carry DK
per claim and polarity, or the full set of distinct sources where retraction is possible.
    Under GOLEM-Iter, the arbiter’s class map of Pattern 6 is an abstraction function from live
claims to the finite model that the gate’s checks run on. The anchors’ sets of integrity incidents
are identity-bounded sets of Pattern 3, kept outside lossy compression.

8.2   Maintenance, recomputation and conformance
A statistic is updated in the same transaction as the records it summarizes, by an event handler
that implements the declared abstract update and nothing else. Forks of a memory, such as
members of a hive, exchange statistic values and merge them by the abstract merge. When the
declared goals change, a store whose compact statistic is too coarse for the new set is rebuilt
from the durable records, which is possible only if the durable records keep the event statistic.
Approximate statistics with L = 1 are recomputed exactly from the records on a schedule
derived from Proposition 4.6. Conformance is checked as in Part I, by replaying recorded events
through the live store and the declared machine [1, Section 3.4], and in addition by sampled
recomputation of stored statistics from the records, with the bound of Section 5.3.

8.3   Certificates, receipts and the gate
The certificate store entries of Part I [1, Appendix C.2] gain three fields for symbolic results:
the theory, the solver with its version, and the digest of the proof log when one exists, which
together make up the TrustedBackend assumption until the proof is checked. Every entry also
records what the result is bound to: the declarations it is about, the machine and the queries it
was checked against, and for a lifted index the view it serves. The module digest covers the
Statistic declaration; the abstraction function from live records to the declared carrier, for
instance from a journal note to the triple of age, closed flag and time since reply, is code under
test as before. Receipts record the hypotheses of the guarantees they cite, such as cR ≤ B, so
that the rate at which a hypothesis fails can itself be estimated. At the GOLEM-Iter gate, a
candidate that replaces a store’s statistic is a persistent memory policy, class A2, and needs
CheckStatistic for every touched consumer, bound to that consumer’s queries and with a

                                                36
MeTTaDinC                                       Omega memory and goals, Part II, 4 November 2026


status the consumer accepts; a candidate that changes a goal needs the checks of Pattern 5
for the new goal set as well as Part I’s classification, including its obligation that a protected
goal keeps its meaning; and Estimated rates enter for empirical consumers, spending from the
confidence budget, with their populations and policies. Through the agent’s interface of Part I
[1, Section 12.4], the agent can ask what its open-item store remembers about an item, and
which goals would lose answers if the store saturated earlier.


9     Toward the worked example
The worked example that follows this part should apply the method end to end to one live
store. The natural candidate is WMTM’s open items and the goal engine on ProtoCosmo2, the
store whose first live review prompted Part I. The protocol has eight steps.
    1. Declare the consumers: the engine’s query (open and at most seven days old), the
       commitment query (open), the follow-up goals in use, and the deduplication key.
    2. Write the item machine on real carriers from the record schema, and the abstraction
       function from journal notes to items: creation time from the opening marker, closing time
       from the first detected closing marker.
    3. Declare the event, joint and engine statistics and the record design the engine uses today,
       and run CheckStatistic, CheckNow and CheckUpdates; a window-based design should
       be refuted with witnesses like those of Pattern 1.
    4. Lift the chosen statistics to the store, and decide which are durable and which live only in
       rebuildable indexes.
    5. For each suggestion, record a receipt with the item’s status from events, the certificate
       it cites and the reviewer’s label when one exists; for a random sample of items, record
       whether the reply detector found the closing reply.
    6. Freeze the receipts as streams pinned by digest.
    7. Estimate the stale-suggestion rate, the detector’s miss rate and, with the window-based
       and event-based designs run side by side in shadow, their paired difference.
    8. Run a mixture monitor on the stale rate with a declared accepted rate and level, and
       report every claim with its status, digests and the confidence it spends.
The data the example needs are a few weeks of journal events with timestamps, the engine’s
suggestion log, and the labels of a live review. The example can then answer a question the first
review could not: how much of the stale-suggestion rate is due to the record design, which the
certified statistics remove, and how much to missed replies, which only better detection reduces.


10      Limits and open problems
Solver verdicts are not checked proofs. The symbolic results of this part are Conditional on
cvc5, and Section 7.2 says which of them could be backed by checked proofs today.
    Sufficiency is relative to input events. A statistic is exactly as good as the events it is
updated with. Missed replies, unresolved aliases of a source and mislabeled polarities are rates
of the running system, and they appear only in receipts.
    Models are not systems. As in Part I, a claim about a declared machine says nothing by
itself about the running code. The abstraction function from live records to the machine’s
carrier is code under test, and conformance replay is evidence about a corpus.

                                                37
MeTTaDinC                                       Omega memory and goals, Part II, 4 November 2026


   Estimated claims rest on stated assumptions. Read as estimates of a rate, the intervals of
Section 5 assume that the receipts share one conditional rate. One-sided claims need only an
order of reading fixed before the labels (Proposition 5.1), but what they average over depends
on the unit of the receipt, and a rate measured under one policy does not carry over to another
without an argument.
   Not every query has a small statistic. Withdrawals defeat identity-bounded sets; exact
counts over a sliding window need the timestamps of every event in the window, and small
summaries of them are approximate [17]; questions about the content of past messages need
the messages.
   Implementations compute in floating point. Proposition 4.6 bounds the effect when the
rounding error per update is stated, and that bound is part of the claim.
    The statistics of this part were designed by hand and then checked. Discovering statistics
automatically on infinite carriers, for instance by abstraction refinement from the witnesses of
failed checks, is open. So is a declaration for continuous mixtures of monitors; the uniform
mixture of Section 5.5 is computed in the bundle’s Python models and cannot yet be written in
the language.
    The language model, the semantic parser and the reply detector remain outside the dec-
larations, as in Part I. Their behavior enters through receipts, estimates and closed-loop
replay.


11    Conclusion
A finite model is where one sees a mechanism clearly, and Part I used finite models to look at
Omega’s memory and goal machinery that way. A running agent’s items age by the second, its
evidence comes from sources nobody enumerated, and its motivational state moves continuously,
so the same questions have to be asked again of state spaces that cannot be listed. A summary
statistic answers, for one store, the question the distinction calculus asks of every memory:
which distinctions have to be kept, and for which consumers. Its identities are short enough
for a solver to check for every state the store can be in. What no declaration reaches, such as
missed replies or what the language model does with a context block, is measured from receipts,
with intervals that stay valid however often the agent looks at them. With these tools an agent
can reason about what its memory keeps while the memory is in use, and can tell, before a goal
changes, what the change will require it to remember. The next step is the worked example:
the open-item store of a live agent, checked and measured by the protocol of Section 9.

C     Reproducing the results
The bundle mettadinc_live_memory_package.zip contains the module file live_memory.
metta; Part I’s module file memory_goals.metta, which it imports from; the registry patch
ls_registry_patch.json and the four patches it builds on; the static checker ex_check.py;
the Python models in models/ and the two Part I models they reuse in part1_models/; the
reference evaluator ls_eval.py; the oracle ls_oracle.py and its results ls_results.json;
and the four frozen streams in streams/. Python 3.11 or later is assumed, with the packages
numpy, scipy, z3-solver and cvc5; the results were produced with z3-solver 5.3.3 and cvc5 1.4.2.

The oracle. python3 ls_oracle.py runs 94 checks in about 10 seconds on one core, prints
one line per check, writes ls_results.json, and exits with a nonzero status if any check fails.
Its first part (56 checks) recomputes every number of this part from the Python models: the
statistical procedures and their simulations, the simulations of the live stores, and the symbolic
obligations in Z3 on encodings written by hand. Its second part (38 checks) reads the module
file with ls_eval.py, answers all 37 requests from the module text with cvc5, including the
two negative fixtures among them, replays every witness by evaluation, compares each verdict
with the first part, runs three differential tests of the module’s maps against the Python models,
and checks that a copy of a stream changed under its declared digest is refused, the third
negative fixture. Symbolic verdicts are reported as Conditional on cvc5; the section verdicts


                                                51
MeTTaDinC                                       Omega memory and goals, Part II, 4 November 2026


of ls_results.json records every verdict with its status, assurance, assumptions and binding,
and Z3’s agreement under agreed_by beside each Conditional one.

The static check. The checker needs the specification’s validation package:

The first pass reports 16 modules, 490 declarations and 129 run requests for the two files, of
which 6 modules, 172 declarations and 37 run requests are this part’s, all conforming to the
registry with the patches. The second pass lists the diagnostics against the unpatched registry,
which are exactly the forms that depend on the patches.

The reference evaluator. ls_eval.py reads the module files with its own reader and
evaluates what the modules use: Cases, Body and Table maps, parameterized maps and
Switch, the arithmetic, comparison and set primitives, Enum, All, Subset, Product and Reach
carriers, For, Like, ActionsOf and imports, statistics, stores, simulations with stuttering, block
partitions with invariants, tolerances and implementation contracts, streams and monitors.
Values are Python values or cvc5 terms, so the same code evaluates a declaration on concrete
states and builds its obligations on symbolic ones. Before it computes anything it checks what a
result will be bound to: the premise of the lifting proposition, the target of a safety transfer and
the digest of every stream it reads. It reports each verdict with the assurance rule of Section 7.2:
Conditional on (TrustedBackend cvc5 ...) when an obligation went to the solver, Checked
when exact evaluation decided it, Refuted with a witness. It is a test aid for this part and not
an implementation of MeTTaDinC.

result means replaying the stored stream. models/make_streams.py records how the two new
streams were generated. live_memory.metta pins three of the streams by digest, and Part I’s
module pins the fourth:
